\chapter{The dataset}
\label{ch:dataset}

The FFVL archive provides flight metadata and recorded trajectories for paragliders
and hang gliders. We describe its coverage, then the cleaning, coordinate conversion
and resampling that produce the trajectories used in the transport analysis.

\section{Data acquisition}
\label{sec:acquisition}

\subsection{Source: the FFVL Coupe F\'ed\'erale de Distance}

The \emph{Coupe F\'ed\'erale de Distance} (CFD) is the cross-country competition
of the French free-flight federation (FFVL). Its two archives separate paragliders
(\texttt{parapente.ffvl.fr}) from hang gliders (\texttt{delta.ffvl.fr}). Each entry
contains flight metadata and, usually, an IGC trajectory.

Seasons are named by their starting year: 1999 means 1999--2000. The paraglider
archive begins in that season; the hang-glider archive begins in 2001--2002.

\subsection{Collected data}

For every season the pipeline retrieves two things:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \textbf{season XML export}, archived verbatim as provenance
        (\texttt{raw\_xml/<year>.xml}); it is the authoritative list of flights and links;
  \item the \textbf{IGC tracklog files} referenced by the XML, one file per flight that
        has a track.
\end{itemize}
From these, a derived \textbf{catalog} is built (\texttt{catalog.csv}, one row per flight,
its columns detailed in Sec.~\ref{sec:catalog}) together with a per-season index
(\texttt{seasons\_index.csv}). The raw data (tens of gigabytes) live on an external disk
and are never committed to the repository. The catalog is itself rebuildable from
the archived XMLs and likewise uncommitted (Sec.~\ref{sec:catalog}).

\subsection{Acquisition and verification}

Two command-line tools, one per discipline, share the same acquisition code. Downloads
are resumable and rate-limited; each response must contain a readable IGC file with an
\texttt{A} header and at least one \texttt{B} fix record (Sec.~\ref{sec:igcformat}).
The archived XMLs preserve the source lists, and the output location is configurable.

An independent check of the files on disk verified all
\num{\StatParaDownloaded} paraglider and \num{\StatHangDownloaded} hang-glider
tracklogs against the same content requirements. Section~\ref{sec:coverage}
compares these downloads with the full list of declared flights.

\subsection{Further data sources}
The present analysis uses the FFVL archive. WeGlide could add sailplanes, and
XContest could broaden paraglider and hang-glider coverage. Both would feed the
same catalog format.

Research-access requests were pending at both portals on 20 July 2026.
At that date, WeGlide's default quota of roughly sixty API requests per day
precluded bulk acquisition. These additional sources are not included here.

\section{The IGC tracklog format}
\label{sec:igcformat}

\begin{revblock}
An \emph{IGC file} is a plain-text flight record with fixed-position fields,
defined by the FAI/IGC specification~\cite{fai_igc_spec}. The first character
identifies the record type:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Record} & \textbf{Content} \\
\midrule
\texttt{A} & Recorder identifier. \\
\texttt{H} & Headers: date, pilot, glider, datum, and related metadata. \\
\texttt{I} & Declaration of optional extension fields. \\
\texttt{B} & UTC time, WGS84 latitude and longitude, GNSS validity flag,
pressure altitude and GNSS altitude (both in metres). \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

The \texttt{B}-record flag describes the GNSS solution: \texttt{A} means a
three-dimensional fix; \texttt{V} means a two-dimensional fix or no GNSS data.
We invalidate GNSS altitude at \texttt{V} fixes and retain their horizontal
coordinates provisionally, pending the checks in Sec.~\ref{sec:fixlevel}.
A pressure value alone does not establish a working barometer.

The parser rejects malformed records: wrong length, illegal times, coordinates or
hemisphere letters. Physically implausible but legally encoded fixes pass to cleaning.
The resulting sequence of fixes has a device-dependent cadence, typically a few
seconds, which need not be uniform.

\end{revblock}

\section{Glider categories}
\label{sec:glider}

\begin{revblock}
Aircraft design changes speed, glide performance and turning geometry,
motivating separate transport analyses~\cite{vilpellet2026}. Paragliders have
a flexible canopy, hang gliders a framed flexible or rigid wing, and sailplanes
a rigid airframe. The FFVL source identifies the first two families; sailplanes
are absent from this dataset.
\end{revblock}

Within each family, \texttt{aile\_class} defines the equipment groups in
Table~\ref{tab:aileclass}. Paraglider EN and legacy LTF codes, such as
\enquote{C ou 2}, are harmonized as EN A--D; CCC and tandem/non-certified entries
remain distinct. Blank and placeholder entries stay unclassified.

EN labels describe safety-test responses and recovery demands, rather than
measured speed or glide ratio~\cite{fpren926_2_2012}. The descriptions here use
the consulted 2012 final draft; the 2013 standard and later amendments were not
compared. FAI hang-glider classes distinguish design and control
systems~\cite{fai_sc7_2024}.

Chapter~\ref{ch:global} uses two operational equipment proxies:
\[
 \text{beginners}:\ \mathrm{EN\ A/B},\qquad
 \text{experts}:\ \mathrm{EN\ C/D/CCC}.
\]
Neither measures individual experience or assumes equally spaced performance.
The harmonization does not reassess certificates, and a tandem label alone does
not establish EN certification.

\section{Data quality and caveats}
\label{sec:dataquality}

A few limitations are already known and will shape the analysis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Flights without a track.} About \SI{8}{\percent} of paraglider entries and
        about \SI{27}{\percent} of hang-glider entries have no IGC file (older or manually
        declared flights); they carry metadata only. These are the flights outside the
        \enquote{With GPS} column of Tables~\ref{tab:seasons-para}
        and~\ref{tab:seasons-hang}.
  \item \textbf{A small residue of unrecoverable files.} A few flights advertise a track
        whose download fails or returns something that is not valid IGC content
        (broken or placeholder links); these are recorded as failures and excluded.
  \item \textbf{Heterogeneous logging.} Sampling rate, availability of the two altitude
        channels, and per-fix positioning noise depend
        on the device; trajectories must be resampled and quality-filtered before any
        ensemble statistic is computed.
  \item \textbf{Altitude channel.} A sizeable minority of loggers record no
        pressure altitude -- almost a third of paraglider and roughly a sixth of hang-glider
        flights. GNSS altitude is used for every admitted flight, with the channel
        recorded per flight (Sec.~\ref{sec:altchannel}).
  \item \textbf{Glider metadata.} Flights are paragliders or hang gliders, tagged by source
        (Section~\ref{sec:glider}); the class field \texttt{aile\_class} must be normalised
        before it is used to stratify.
  \item \textbf{Incomplete dates.} Some historical entries carry the placeholder date
        \texttt{0000-00-00}, written as a sentinel at acquisition. Such entries cannot
        support a calendar-conditioned comparison until a date is recovered independently;
        the relative-time preprocessing does not itself enforce this metadata exclusion.
        The count is small: \num{\StatCatParaPlaceholderDateCount} of
        \num{\StatCatParaFlights} paraglider entries carry the sentinel
        (\StatCatParaPlaceholderDatePct\,\%), \num{\StatCatHangPlaceholderDateCount} of
        \num{\StatCatHangFlights} hang-glider ones, and the largest within-season fraction is
        only \StatCatParaPlaceholderWorstPct\,\% of its entries
        (\StatCatParaPlaceholderWorstSeason).
\end{itemize}
These caveats motivate the pre-processing described in the remainder of this chapter.

\section{Trajectory pre-processing}
\label{sec:preproc}
Each IGC file is parsed into a sequence of fixes $(t_i,\varphi_i,\lambda_i,h_i)$ -- UTC
time, geodetic latitude and longitude, and altitude. The pipeline then proceeds in a fixed order:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), itemsep=1pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item choose the altitude channel (Sec.~\ref{sec:altchannel});
  \item clean individual fixes (Sec.~\ref{sec:fixlevel});
  \item trim the ground phases (Sec.~\ref{sec:trimming});
  \item filter out unfit whole flights (Sec.~\ref{sec:flightfilter});
  \item convert to a local Cartesian frame (Sec.~\ref{sec:enu});
  \item enforce a uniform sampling interval within each flight (Sec.~\ref{sec:uniform});
  \item smooth and differentiate the trajectory (Sec.~\ref{sec:savgol}).
\end{enumerate}
The output consists of fix, segment and attempted-flight tables, accompanied by a
table of suspect intervals retained for later sensitivity analysis. What each file holds and how the disk is organised is set out in
Sec.~\ref{sec:pipelinemap}.

Steps~(i)--(iv) use raw geographic coordinates: great-circle distances give horizontal
speeds, and altitude is read as logged. Step~(v) places the cleaned, trimmed trajectory
in a local East--North--Up frame, anchored at its first surviving fix. Resampling and
differentiation then act on Cartesian components.

Table~\ref{tab:workingparams} collects the working thresholds. They are specified
independently of the transport exponents; Table~\ref{tab:pipecensus} records their
effect on retention.

\subsection{Choice of altitude channel}
\label{sec:altchannel}

The analysis uses \emph{logged GNSS altitude} throughout: $z=h_{\mathrm{GNSS}}$.
This keeps one channel across flights but leaves the recorder's vertical datum unresolved:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Recorder specification} & \textbf{GNSS height convention} \\
\midrule
IGC-approved & Ellipsoidal height~\cite{fai_igc_spec}. \\
CIVL hang-/paragliding & Geoid-referenced height in the consulted document,
which contains draft annotations~\cite{fai_civl_s7h_2024}. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
Compliance has not been established across this historical archive, and the parser
preserves the numerical field. Absolute-height comparisons therefore need a datum audit.

A constant height offset cancels from increments; spatially varying geoid corrections
and changing sensor biases do not. Pressure altitude also depends on atmospheric
pressure and temperature. Using GNSS consistently does not make it uniformly more accurate.

\paragraph{Comparing the barometric and GNSS channels.}
From \num{3000} sampled raw flights per discipline, we retain those with at least
95\% pressure-altitude presence and median cadence within \SI{0.25}{\second}
of the pooled mode (median intervals rounded to seconds).

Each pair of spectra uses the same longest block of finite, nonzero readings with
valid GNSS flags. Duplicate/backward timestamps and gaps above 1.5 modal intervals
break the block; ties in fix count select the earliest block.
Linear resampling at the modal interval requires at least 256 raw and 256 grid fixes.

Figure~\ref{fig:altnoise} shows a time-varying channel difference: a constant offset
does not align the \SI{30}{\minute} traces. The high-frequency median spectra
nearly coincide, with broad variation between flights. These comparisons exclude
missing-channel runs and do not represent every sampled recorder.
Appendix~\ref{app:psd} defines the spectral estimator.

\paragraph{Variation between flights.}
Similar medians can conceal different upper tails. We therefore measure each flight's
median PSD in the \SIrange{0.35}{0.50}{\hertz} band and compare it with the median of
these values across barometric records.
Of the \num{\StatAltNoisePsdSampleFlights} paired flights contributing to the spectrum, \StatAltNoiseGnssExcessThreeXPct\,\% carry a GNSS floor above three times
the barometric channel's typical one, and \StatAltNoiseGnssExcessTenXPct\,\% above ten
times it. Multipath is one possible GNSS error source
\cite{garciacrespillo2024}; this comparison does not attribute the measured excess
to a unique physical mechanism.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to the observation interval.}
A same-flight comparison separates the selection of valid intervals from subsequent
trajectory smoothing. Using the same decoder and Welch estimator, we compare a
whole-record rule, which can retain missing-value codes and interpolate across
recording gaps, with the paired valid-block rule above. On the same \num{2008}
flights, the 90th percentile of the per-flight GNSS spectral floor decreases from
\num{3.758} to \num{0.590}\,\si{\metre\squared\per\hertz}; the corresponding
barometric values change from \num{0.545} to \num{0.508}\,\si{\metre\squared\per\hertz}.
Here the floor is each flight's median PSD over \SIrange{0.35}{0.50}{\hertz};
its percentile is distinct from a per-frequency percentile in
Fig.~\ref{fig:altnoise}.

Neither comparison uses the pipeline's Savitzky--Golay output: both use logged
altitudes, linear resampling and Welch detrending. The smaller GNSS upper tail
therefore reflects the changed observation interval in this comparison. It does
not demonstrate lower physical sensor noise. The experiment changes the combined
validity, missing-value and gap-selection rule, without identifying the contribution
of each criterion separately. These spectra remain conditional on recorders and
intervals with adequate paired-channel support.

\paragraph{Availability.}
A flight needs finite, nonzero GNSS altitude at
\SI{\PreprocGnssPresentMinPct}{\percent} of its fixes and an altitude range of at least
\SI{\PreprocGnssMinRangeM}{\metre}. These criteria reject incomplete or nearly constant
channels, including genuine flights with too little vertical excursion. No pressure
altitude is substituted.

\medskip
The presence criterion excludes \StatScanParaNoGnssPct\,\% of paraglider and
\StatScanHangNoGnssPct\,\% of hang-glider flights. Both channels fall below their
presence thresholds in \StatScanParaNoAltitudePct\,\% and
\StatScanHangNoAltitudePct\,\%, respectively. Within admitted flights, missing GNSS
altitude initially leaves the horizontal fix intact. At resampling, short altitude
holes are reconstructed and flagged; long holes split the full trajectory
(Sec.~\ref{sec:uniform}). Missing readings affect
\StatScanParaGnssMissFlightsPct\,\% of paraglider and
\StatScanHangGnssMissFlightsPct\,\% of hang-glider flights, with a median of
\StatScanParaGnssMissMedianFixes\ missing altitudes among affected paraglider flights.

\paragraph{Local barometric support.}
The pressure channel has a separate role: it can corroborate a suspected frozen GNSS
position and an interior ground interval (Secs.~\ref{sec:fixlevel} and
\ref{sec:trimming}). Its flight-level eligibility requires at least
\SI{\PreprocBaroWitnessPresentMinPct}{\percent} nonzero finite readings and a range of at
least \SI{\PreprocBaroWitnessMinRangeM}{\metre}. The corresponding archive fractions,
\StatPipeParaBaroWitnessPct\,\% and \StatPipeHangBaroWitnessPct\,\%, describe eligibility,
not coverage of every candidate interval.

At fix $i$, define $b_i=1$ when a finite, nonzero pressure altitude accompanies the
horizontal position in the same decoded record, and $b_i=0$ otherwise. For a candidate
interval $I$, the barometric test is available only if
\begin{equation}
  B_I=B_{\mathrm{flight}}\prod_{i\in I}b_i=1.
  \label{eq:baro-coverage}
\end{equation}
Zero pressure values are treated as missing before duplicate timestamps are merged:
the pair $\{0,1000\}$ therefore supplies one \SI{1000}{\metre} reading. No temporal
interpolation supplies a witness. Equation~\eqref{eq:baro-coverage} requires pressure
and position in every record of $I$, even when GNSS altitude is invalid.

An eligible barometer with incomplete local coverage causes the barometric test to
abstain. Flights without an eligible barometer remain in the analysis, subject to
the weaker recorder-declaration rule below. Restricting a sensitivity analysis to
$B_I=1$ would measure the effect of this difference in evidence.

\subsection{Fix-level cleaning}
\label{sec:fixlevel}

Fix-level cleaning distinguishes three cases (Fig.~\ref{fig:defect-schematic}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item An \emph{off-the-trend defect}, such as a horizontal spike, may receive a
        Hampel flag. The separate impossibility gate decides deletion.
  \item An \emph{on-the-trend defect}, such as a duplicate timestamp or frozen
        position, requires timestamp or recorder checks.
  \item A \emph{vertical defect} invalidates altitude while provisionally retaining
        the horizontal fix.
\end{itemize}
These local tests leave slowly varying and plausible-looking errors unresolved.
Deletions, boundaries and reconstruction flags record what the pipeline changed.

\paragraph{Geometry on the raw coordinates.}
The cleaning runs on the raw geodetic coordinates $(\varphi_i,\lambda_i)$, before the
Cartesian conversion (Sec.~\ref{sec:enu}), so distances between fixes are great-circle
(haversine) distances~\cite{sklearn_haversine} on a sphere of mean radius
$R_\oplus=\SI{6371}{\kilo\metre}$:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:haversine}
\Delta s_i = 2R_\oplus\arcsin\sqrt{\sin^2\!\frac{\varphi_{i+1}-\varphi_i}{2}
  + \cos\varphi_i\cos\varphi_{i+1}\,\sin^2\!\frac{\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i}{2}}\,,
\end{equation}
The same distances give step velocities, total path length and extent:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 v_{xy,i}&=\frac{\Delta s_i}{t_{i+1}-t_i}, &
 v_{z,i}&=\frac{h_{i+1}-h_i}{t_{i+1}-t_i},\\
 L&=\sum_i\Delta s_i, &
 D&=\max_i\Delta s(\mathrm{fix}_0,\mathrm{fix}_i).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:raw-track-geometry}
\end{equation}
Near $45^\circ$ latitude, the spherical distance error is at most about $0.3\%$:
\SI{3}{\centi\metre} on a \SI{10}{\metre} step, below metre-scale GNSS noise.
Appendix~\ref{app:geodesy} derives the ellipsoidal correction and its latitude dependence.

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{The Hampel identifier.}
Let the window of fix $k$ be the fixes within $\pm w$ of $t_k$, and
$\tilde\varphi_k,\tilde\lambda_k$ the component-wise window medians. The residual is the
great-circle distance from that median point,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hampelresid}
  r_k=\Delta s\big((\varphi_k,\lambda_k),(\tilde\varphi_k,\tilde\lambda_k)\big),
\end{equation}
with local scale $\operatorname{MAD}_k=\operatorname{med}\{r_j:|t_j-t_k|\le w\}$, and the
fix is flagged when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hampel}
  r_k > \max\!\big(k\,\sigma_k,\ \varepsilon_{\min}\big),
  \qquad \sigma_k = 1.4826\operatorname{MAD}_k,
\end{equation}
$\sigma_k$ a robust local scale~\cite{rousseeuw1993}. The factor $1.4826$ is the
usual one-dimensional Gaussian MAD normalization; for these two-dimensional radial
residuals it is a scale convention, not an exact standard deviation or a calibrated
false-alarm probability.

The working values are $k=\PreprocHampelK$,
$w=\SI{\PreprocHampelWindowS}{\second}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\min}=\SI{\PreprocHampelEpsM}{\metre}$, with at least
\PreprocHampelMinFixes\ fixes per window.

Flags contribute to the anomaly count;
they never gate deletion. A thermalling circle can depart from its local median
while remaining reachable at the absolute speed bound.

\end{revblock}

\paragraph{Reach, rejoin, or cut.}
Write $a$ for the last fix the scan accepted, the \emph{anchor}, and measure every
candidate against the chord from it,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:reach}
  V(a,i)=\frac{\Delta s\big((\varphi_a,\lambda_a),(\varphi_i,\lambda_i)\big)}{t_i-t_a}.
\end{equation}
An unreachable fix opens a pending block. Its anchor $a$ stays fixed while
elapsed time grows. The scan then applies three actions:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}p{.33\linewidth} X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Action} \\
\midrule
Reachable fix, no pending block & Keep it and advance the anchor. \\
First rejoin within $w$ & Delete the pending predecessors; keep the rejoin as the new anchor. \\
No rejoin within $w$ & Keep the block, split at its first fix and re-anchor on the current fix. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The cap starts at the pending block's first fix. It reuses the Hampel half-window
without a shared calibration. Gaps left by deletion are bridged or split at
resampling (Sec.~\ref{sec:uniform}).

Figure~\ref{fig:reach-gate} compares a spike, a moderate offset and a large offset.
The moderate offset becomes reachable as time grows and can cost genuine fixes;
the large offset remains unreachable and is split. This gate cannot identify
which branch contains the true positions.

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{Vertical-speed checks.}
Write the observed altitude as $h_i=z_i+\epsilon_i$. Even a smooth true trajectory
produces noisy finite differences,
\begin{equation}
 v_{z,i}^{\mathrm{obs}}=\frac{h_{i+1}-h_i}{t_{i+1}-t_i}
 =\overline v_{z,i}^{\mathrm{true}}+
   \frac{\epsilon_{i+1}-\epsilon_i}{t_{i+1}-t_i}.
 \label{eq:altitude-difference-noise}
\end{equation}
For independent errors of variance $\sigma_z^2$, the differenced noise has variance
$2\sigma_z^2/\Delta t^2$. Short cadences therefore amplify altitude error.
This calculation illustrates the effect; real GNSS errors may be correlated.

The sustained-excess statistic is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:vzlocal}
 \tilde v_j=\operatorname{med}\{ |v_{z,i}^{\mathrm{obs}}|:
       |m_i-m_j|\le w_v,\ v_{z,i}^{\mathrm{obs}}\text{ finite}\},\qquad
 m_i=\tfrac12(t_i+t_{i+1}),
\end{equation}
with $w_v=\SI{\PreprocVzWindowS}{\second}$. The statistic measures local speed
magnitude. Each of the $N-1$ steps is assigned
to its midpoint $m_i$. Figure~\ref{fig:vz-window} illustrates window selection and
the median: for $2r+1$ finite values, at least $r+1$ must exceed the bound for the
median to do so. For an even count, the two central values are averaged.

With fewer than \PreprocVzMinWindowFixes\ finite steps, the implementation does
not use the median. Writing $n_j$ for that count, the quantity used by the cleaning is
\[
 u_j=\begin{cases}
       \tilde v_j, & n_j\geq\PreprocVzMinWindowFixes,\\
       |v_{z,j}^{\mathrm{obs}}|, & n_j<\PreprocVzMinWindowFixes.
     \end{cases}
\]
The second branch is the sparse-window fallback. It can occur at a slow recording
cadence, near missing altitudes, or at the ends of a record. The sustained-excess
rule marks the altitude of an interior fix $k$ as missing when
\begin{equation}
  u_{k-1}>v_z^{\max}\quad\text{and}\quad u_k>v_z^{\max},
  \qquad v_z^{\max}=\SI{\PreprocMaxVSpeedMps}{\metre\per\second}.
  \label{eq:vz-censor}
\end{equation}
Both the incoming and outgoing window statistics must exceed the bound strictly.
With populated windows, the rule can invalidate altitude even when the two steps
touching fix $k$ are below the bound (Fig.~\ref{fig:vz-decision}). The overlapping
windows locate a neighbourhood of excessive speeds; they do not identify its
erroneous altitude independently.

\paragraph{Unreturned vertical steps.}
An isolated jump without a prompt return can escape both checks. Define
\begin{align}
 e_i&=\mathbf1\{|v_{z,i}^{\mathrm{obs}}|>v_z^{\max}\},
 &J_i&=|h_{i+1}-h_i|,\\
 d_i^{\mathrm{return}}&=
 \min_{\substack{j=i+1,\ldots,i+5\\h_j\ \mathrm{finite}}}|h_j-h_i|.
\end{align}
The minimum uses the next five fix positions, fewer at the record end,
and equals $+\infty$ if none has finite altitude. Set $e_i=0$ outside the observed
steps. The counter
\path{n_alt_level_shift} records candidates satisfying
\begin{equation}
 e_i=1,\qquad e_{i-1}=e_{i+1}=0,\qquad
 d_i^{\mathrm{return}}>0.3J_i.
 \label{eq:level-shift-candidate}
\end{equation}
This short return check does not establish a permanent offset. It runs before
trimming and segment selection, and can overlap other altitude rules.

Among retained-flight metadata, 47,440 paraglider and 1,350 hang-glider records
contain at least one candidate. Reprocessing eight previously selected examples
with the current pipeline reproduces their counters. In each discipline these
include two high-count records and two seeded positive-count records. All eight
still have candidate times near usable phase-feature centres. This is temporal
association, not proof that an uncorrected sensor defect enters a feature. These counts describe candidates before
later cuts, rather than surviving confirmed defects. Automatic repair is not applied:
a level shift and a real large increment can have similar signatures. Their effect
on phase features still requires a sensitivity analysis.

Figure~\ref{fig:fixlevel}b motivates the working bound
\SI{\PreprocMaxVSpeedMps}{\metre\per\second}: around
\SIrange{9}{10}{\metre\per\second}, the density begins to decay more slowly and
the paraglider curve crosses the hang-glider curve. The transition is gradual.
The bound is an empirical cleaning choice, so extreme genuine descents can be
invalidated too. In the full diagnostic sample, \SI{0.069}{\percent} of window
medians exceed it. This exceedance fraction differs from the removal rate, which
also depends on neighbouring windows and the spike rule.

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{Deletion and mandatory boundaries.}
Altitude rules retain timestamp and horizontal coordinates; position and timestamp
rules may remove the whole fix. Reconstruction is deferred to resampling.
After all cleaning checks, every surviving step above the horizontal speed bound
becomes a mandatory segment boundary, counted as \path{n_boundaried}.
This also catches an impossible return from a block that the forward scan kept.
\end{revblock}

\rev{The forward scan does not re-evaluate its initial anchor, so an erroneous first
fix can survive this local procedure and displace the origin of the later Cartesian
frame. A separate flight-level test therefore rejects a track containing a fix beyond
the distance reachable from its first retained position
(Sec.~\ref{sec:flightfilter}). The reach test acts on
spherical distances before projection and smoothing, with a \SI{50}{\metre}
tolerance. The written Cartesian trajectories undergo separate diagnostic checks
(Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset-checks}); those operations do not preserve the reach inequality
as an exact invariant.}

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{Frozen-position runs and the evidence for GNSS lock loss.}
A logger may repeat its last position after losing GNSS lock while timestamps continue.
An unremoved run then resembles a period of spatial confinement. The detector uses
three joint conditions,
\begin{equation}
 \label{eq:frozenlock}
 D_I<\delta_{xy},\qquad t_{\max I}-t_{\min I}\ge\tau_{\mathrm{freeze}},\qquad W_I=1,
\end{equation}
where $D_I$ is the great-circle diagonal of the latitude--longitude bounding box of
candidate run $I$, evaluated after longitude unwrapping. This is the implemented
extent proxy, not an exact all-pairs diameter. The working values are
$\delta_{xy}=\SI{\PreprocFrozenEpsM}{\metre}$ and
$\tau_{\mathrm{freeze}}=\SI{\PreprocFrozenTauS}{\second}$.

Let $E_I$ denote exact equality of both coordinate fields throughout the run,
$d_I$ the fraction of its fixes with a \texttt{V} flag or missing GNSS altitude, and
$\Delta b_I$ the median pressure altitude of the final quarter minus that of the first
quarter. With $B_I$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:baro-coverage}, the witness is
\begin{equation}
 W_I=E_I\ \lor\
 \begin{cases}
  |\Delta b_I|<\delta_z, & B_I=1,\\
  \mathrm{false}, & B_{\mathrm{flight}}=1,\ B_I=0,\\
  d_I>1/2, & B_{\mathrm{flight}}=0,
 \end{cases}
 \qquad \delta_z=\SI{\PreprocFrozenDeltaZM}{\metre}.
 \label{eq:frozen-witness}
\end{equation}
Removed frozen-lock runs always split the trajectory. Exact coordinate repetition
is a separate witness; otherwise incomplete local pressure coverage forces abstention.

The signatures have limits. Quantized coordinates can repeat for a stationary receiver,
and sparse fixes do not establish continuous stillness between them. Small net pressure
change can hide an excursion; a barometer can also continue climbing during a horizontal
GNSS freeze. Shared logger failures can affect both channels.

For scale, a coordinated turn in still air has
\begin{equation}
 R=\frac{v_{\mathrm{air}}^2}{g\tan\phi},\qquad
 T_{\mathrm{turn}}=\frac{2\pi R}{v_{\mathrm{air}}}.
 \label{eq:turn-radius-cleaning}
\end{equation}
At $v_{\mathrm{air}}=\SI{10}{\metre\per\second}$ and bank angle $\phi=35^\circ$,
$2R\simeq\SI{29}{\metre}$ exceeds $\delta_{xy}$, while a sustained
\SI{1}{\metre\per\second} climb changes pressure altitude far more than $\delta_z$.
These are reference scales, not universal minimum diameters or guarantees for every
wind, bank angle and recorder cadence.

A genuine small manoeuvre can satisfy all signatures and be cut; a jittering freeze
without a witness can survive. Runs shorter than $\tau_{\mathrm{freeze}}$ are retained.
Independent inspection or labelled injections are needed to estimate these error rates.

\end{revblock}

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{Integrity and validation.}
The integrity gate rejects flights whose cleaning burden exceeds
\SI{\PreprocIntegrityMaxPct}{\percent}. Counts use only the airborne window selected
by trimming (Sec.~\ref{sec:trimming}), so discarded ground recordings do not affect
this decision. Let $N_{\mathrm{del}}$ count deleted fixes,
$N_{\mathrm{keep}}$ surviving fixes and $N_{z\mathrm{bad}}$ survivors whose altitude
was invalidated. The burden is
\begin{equation}
 f_{\mathrm{clean}}=
 \frac{N_{\mathrm{del}}+N_{z\mathrm{bad}}}
      {N_{\mathrm{keep}}+N_{\mathrm{del}}}.
 \label{eq:cleaning-burden}
\end{equation}
Altitude invalidation includes out-of-band values, sustained excess, spikes and
\texttt{V} declarations. Duplicate-second merges and later reconstruction are excluded.
This gate removes \num{\StatPipeParaDropCleaningRebuiltTooMuch} paraglider and
\num{\StatPipeHangDropCleaningRebuiltTooMuch} hang-glider flights.
The larger loss occurs later when no segment survives resampling:
\num{\StatPipeParaDropNoSegmentSurvived} and
\num{\StatPipeHangDropNoSegmentSurvived} flights, respectively.

Removal fractions measure the amount changed; threshold sweeps measure sensitivity.
Classification error rates require independent labels. Synthetic regressions and
the real examples in Fig.~\ref{fig:cleaning-real} check specific failure modes,
while a representative labelled archive audit remains to be done.

\subsection{Trimming of ground phases}
\label{sec:trimming}
\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{Outer ground phases (take-off and landing).}
The logger starts recording before take-off and stops after landing; these ground phases
must be stripped before any kinematic analysis, using the horizontal speed $v_{xy}$ alone.
With $v_0=\SI{\PreprocTakeoffSpeedMps}{\metre\per\second}$ and
$T_0=\SI{\PreprocSustainedS}{\second}$ (Table~\ref{tab:workingparams}), the retained
airborne segment is $[t_{\mathrm{on}},t_{\mathrm{off}}]$ with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trimming}
  t_{\mathrm{on}}=\min\bigl\{t:\ v_{xy}>v_0\ \text{on}\ [t,\,t+T_0]\bigr\},
  \qquad
  t_{\mathrm{off}}=\max\bigl\{t:\ v_{xy}>v_0\ \text{on}\ [t-T_0,\,t]\bigr\},
\end{equation}
the same sustained-speed rule read forward and time-reversed. Here $v_{xy}$ is the
speed inferred on each observed step, not a continuously measured velocity.
Interior slow periods
do not define these outer boundaries, although slow flight at either end can be clipped. A flight with no sustained
stretch at all is discarded.

The persistence time $T_0$ reduces sensitivity to brief speed excursions. It does
not bound take-off or landing error by $T_0$: a sustained fast ground movement can be
retained, while a long airborne interval below $v_0$ at either end can be removed.
The trimmed fraction is recorded per flight, and such cases require direct inspection.

The median time removed before onset and after landing is
\SI{\StatTrimParaTakeoffMedianS}{\second} and
\SI{\StatTrimParaLandingMedianS}{\second} for paragliders, and
\SI{\StatTrimHangTakeoffMedianS}{\second} and
\SI{\StatTrimHangLandingMedianS}{\second} for hang gliders
(Fig.~\ref{fig:trim-split}). Counts cover
\num{\StatTrimParaScanCount} and \num{\StatTrimHangScanCount} flights reaching a
defined window. The stored \path{trimmed_fraction} combines the two ends.

\paragraph{Interior ground intervals.}
A landing and relaunch in one log can imitate a long waiting episode. The detector
requires sustained low horizontal speed on an interval $I$ containing at least
three fixes, all with paired barometry. Fit
$h_i^{\mathrm{baro}}=\widehat\alpha+\widehat\beta t_i+r_i$ by least squares.
The joint conditions are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 v_{xy}<v_0\ \text{on }I,\qquad |I|\ge T_{\mathrm{ground}},\qquad B_I=1,\\
 Q_{.95}(r)-Q_{.05}(r)\le\delta_z^{\mathrm{gr}},\qquad
 |\widehat\beta|\le\delta_z/\tau_{\mathrm{freeze}},
 \end{gathered}
 \label{eq:interior-ground}
\end{equation}
where $|I|$ denotes duration,
$T_{\mathrm{ground}}=\SI{\PreprocInteriorGroundMin}{\minute}$ and
$\delta_z^{\mathrm{gr}}=\SI{\PreprocGroundFlatnessM}{\metre}$.
The residual span tolerates isolated extremes; the slope bound prevents detrending
from erasing a steady climb. Without complete eligible barometry the detector
abstains, since slow ground-relative flight can otherwise resemble a landing.

Excised intervals split the trajectory. Subsequent flight-level cuts apply to the
parent flight, summing duration and path length over its remaining blocks;
they are not reapplied separately to each segment.

Interior excisions occur in \StatTrimParaInteriorNonzeroCount\ paraglider flights
(\StatTrimParaInteriorNonzeroPct\,\%) and \StatTrimHangInteriorNonzeroCount\
hang-glider flights (\StatTrimHangInteriorNonzeroPct\,\%). Most affected flights
have one excision; the maxima are \StatTrimParaInteriorMax\ and
\StatTrimHangInteriorMax\ (Fig.~\ref{fig:trim-interior}).

\end{revblock}

\subsection{Flight-level filtering}
\label{sec:flightfilter}
\begin{revblock}
The cuts below define the analysed population. They act on the trimmed parent
flight, with duration $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$ and path $L$ accumulated only within
contiguous blocks. Forced boundaries and gaps longer than $g_{\max}$ contribute
to neither sum; elapsed span $T_{\mathrm{span}}$ includes them.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l l X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Quantity} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
Recorded duration & $T_{\mathrm{rec}}\ge\SI{\PreprocMinDurationMin}{\minute}$ & Exclude short records. \\
Elapsed span & $T_{\mathrm{span}}\le\SI{\PreprocMaxDurationH}{\hour}$ & Screen long recording sessions. \\
Path length & $L\ge\SI{\PreprocMinPathKm}{\kilo\metre}$ & Require horizontal travel. \\
GNSS altitude range & $\max z-\min z\ge\SI{\PreprocMinAltRangeM}{\metre}$ & Require vertical excursion. \\
Mean speed & $L/T_{\mathrm{rec}}\le v_{xy}^{\max}$ & Check consistency with step bounds. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
These are sample restrictions, not a soaring classifier: a genuine flight with
little vertical excursion can fail. Mean speed adds no independent evidence when
all included steps already satisfy the bound. Figure~\ref{fig:preproc} shows
sample-size sensitivity; a plateau does not establish unbiased selection.

\midrule
Paragliders & \num{\StatScanParaShortPathCount} & \num{\StatScanParaLongEnoughCount} \\
Hang gliders & \num{\StatScanHangShortPathCount} & \num{\StatScanHangLongEnoughCount} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

These raw counts are distinct from the sequential post-trimming removal counts.

\paragraph{Reach from the first fix.}
Before projection and smoothing, every fix must satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \Delta s(\mathrm{fix}_0,\mathrm{fix}_i)
 \le v_{xy}^{\max}t_i+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{reach}},
 \qquad \varepsilon_{\mathrm{reach}}=\SI{50}{\metre},\qquad \forall i,
 \label{eq:flight-reach}
\end{equation}
with $t_i$ measured from the first trimmed fix. Testing each elapsed time prevents
a later long duration from excusing an early impossible displacement.
It also catches a corrupt initial anchor whose first step is no longer summed into $L$.

This rejects \num{\StatPipeParaDropExtentOutOfReachOfTheFirstFix} paraglider and
\num{\StatPipeHangDropExtentOutOfReachOfTheFirstFix} hang-glider flights in the
stored snapshot. The written Cartesian trajectories receive separate checks with
operational tolerances (Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset-checks}).

Unclassified equipment remains in pooled analyses but is excluded from class-resolved
comparisons (Sec.~\ref{sec:glider}). Sampling regularity is tested after resampling
(Sec.~\ref{sec:uniform}).

\end{revblock}

\subsection{From geographic to local Cartesian coordinates}
\label{sec:enu}
\rev{The cleaned, trimmed track is next mapped from geographic to local Cartesian
coordinates: resampling and smoothing operate on the coordinates \emph{componentwise}
(Sec.~\ref{sec:preproc}), and latitudes and longitudes cannot play that role, being angles
on a curved surface whose componentwise differences are not displacements. The mapping
proceeds in two steps.}

\paragraph{Step 1: geodetic to ECEF.} Each fix is converted to Earth-Centred,
Earth-Fixed (ECEF) coordinates on the WGS84 ellipsoid (semi-major axis
$a=\SI{6378137}{\metre}$, flattening $f=1/298.257223563$, $e^2=f(2-f)$)~\cite{nga_wgs84,esa_navipedia_ellipsoidal_cartesian},
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ecef}
N(\varphi)=\frac{a}{\sqrt{1-e^2\sin^2\varphi}},\qquad
\begin{aligned}
X &= (N+h)\cos\varphi\cos\lambda,\\
Y &= (N+h)\cos\varphi\sin\lambda,\\
Z &= \big(N(1-e^2)+h\big)\sin\varphi,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$N(\varphi)$ the prime-vertical radius of curvature and $h$ the ellipsoidal height along
the normal, derived step by step in Appendix~\ref{app:geodesy}.

\paragraph{Geodetic versus geocentric latitude.}
WGS84 reports geodetic latitude $\varphi$, measured along the ellipsoid normal.
Geocentric latitude $\psi$ follows the radius from the Earth's centre
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ellipsoid}). Their difference is approximately
\[
 \varphi-\psi\simeq\frac{e^2}{2}\sin 2\varphi,
 \qquad \max|\varphi-\psi|\simeq0.19^\circ.
\]
Confusing them can shift a position by about \SI{20}{\kilo\metre}
(Appendix~\ref{app:geodesy}). Equation~\eqref{eq:ecef} already takes the
geodetic latitude recorded in IGC; no latitude conversion is needed.

\paragraph{Step 2: ECEF to local ENU.} \rev{Taking the first fix $(\varphi_0,\lambda_0,h_0)$
of the \emph{trimmed} track as the origin --- close to but not the take-off point, since
the ground phase has been removed (Sec.~\ref{sec:trimming}) --- the ECEF displacement
$\Delta\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{X}_0$ is rotated into the local East--North--Up (ENU)
frame tangent to the ellipsoid at that point,}
\begin{equation}
\begin{pmatrix}E\\N\\U\end{pmatrix}=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin\lambda_0 & \cos\lambda_0 & 0\\
-\sin\varphi_0\cos\lambda_0 & -\sin\varphi_0\sin\lambda_0 & \cos\varphi_0\\
\cos\varphi_0\cos\lambda_0 & \cos\varphi_0\sin\lambda_0 & \sin\varphi_0
\end{pmatrix}
\Delta\mathbf{X},
\end{equation}
The rotation is orthogonal with unit determinant: it preserves the three-dimensional
ECEF chord and angles (Appendix~\ref{app:geodesy}). Keeping only $(E,N)$ then
projects the chord onto the tangent plane.

For a point at surface distance $d$ from the origin on a sphere,
\begin{equation}
 r_{EN}=R_\oplus\sin(d/R_\oplus),\qquad
 d-r_{EN}=\frac{d^3}{6R_\oplus^2}+O(d^5/R_\oplus^4).
 \label{eq:enu-projection-error}
\end{equation}
The horizontal shortening is about \SI{0.03}{\metre} at \SI{20}{\kilo\metre} and
\SI{0.51}{\metre} at \SI{50}{\kilo\metre}; it reaches about \SI{33}{\metre} at
\SI{200}{\kilo\metre}. The often-used $d^3/(24R_\oplus^2)$ expression instead compares
an arc with its full three-dimensional chord. Neither estimate is a uniform error
bound for every pair of fixes in a fixed frame.

The height input is the logged GNSS altitude, whose datum has not been harmonized.

\[
 |\delta\mathbf r_{EN}|\sim |\delta h|\frac{d}{R_\oplus}.
\]

origin altitude. The rotated component $U$ also contains Earth curvature and is
not used for vertical dynamics. Velocities depend on altitude increments;
absolute heights retain the datum limitation of Sec.~\ref{sec:altchannel}.

\subsection{Uniform sampling within a flight}
\label{sec:uniform}

observations. Each flight keeps its native interval,
$\Delta t=\operatorname{median}_i(t_{i+1}-t_i)$: a flight recorded every second
stays at one second, and a flight recorded every five seconds stays at five seconds.

\paragraph{Fill a short gap; split at a long one.}
For a gap $g=t_{i+1}-t_i$ between surviving fixes, interpolation is allowed when
$g\le g_{\max}$, with
\begin{equation}
 g_{\max}=\min\!\left(
   \PreprocMaxGapFactor\,\Delta t,\;
   \max\!\left(\SI{\PreprocMaxGapS}{\second},\,2\Delta t\right)\right).
 \label{eq:uniform-gap-bound}
\end{equation}
At fast cadences the relative limit binds; at slower cadences the absolute cap
binds, with a $2\Delta t$ floor allowing one missed fix. Thus the configured
\SI{\PreprocMaxGapS}{\second} value is not an absolute ceiling for every logger:
at $\Delta t=\SI{15}{\second}$, the bound is \SI{30}{\second}. For example,
\[
 \Delta t=\SI{1}{\second}\ \Rightarrow\ g_{\max}=\SI{10}{\second},
 \qquad
 \Delta t=\SI{5}{\second}\ \Rightarrow\ g_{\max}=\SI{20}{\second}.
\]
A longer gap starts a new segment. The same bound applies between consecutive
finite altitude readings, even with horizontal fixes in between. Cleaning
boundaries always force a split. The cap limits reconstruction but cannot ensure
that manoeuvres inside short gaps are resolved.

\paragraph{Reconstructing the coordinates.}
Within segment $s$, the grid is $t_k^{(s)}=t_{\mathrm{start}}^{(s)}+k\Delta t$,
ending at or before its last fix. An already uniform, complete series is unchanged.
Otherwise, $E$ and $N$ are evaluated with a shape-preserving cubic interpolant
(PCHIP)~\cite{fritsch1984}, and $z$ with linear interpolation. Linear filling avoids overshoot in altitude
but can smooth away a climb--glide transition. Missing-altitude prefixes and suffixes
are excluded before the grid is built; altitude is never extended beyond finite support.

For altitude missing at existing fixes, let $g_z$ separate the surrounding finite
altitude readings. The rule is
\[
 \begin{cases}
  g_z\le g_{\max}: & \text{fill altitude linearly},\\
  g_z>g_{\max}: & \text{exclude intervening fixes and split the full trajectory}.
 \end{cases}
\]
It applies to both missing raw readings and values invalidated by cleaning
(Fig.~\ref{fig:uniform-sampling-schematic}c). Unsupported intervals remain
recorded as rejected segment candidates. Sacrificing their horizontal coverage
avoids constructing long unsupported three-dimensional paths.

Three flags record reconstruction for the phase analysis
(Chapter~\ref{ch:phases}):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}l X@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Flag} & \textbf{Marked grid times} \\
\midrule
\texttt{interpolated} & No surviving fix within $\Delta t/2$. \\
\texttt{z\_reconstructed} & The above, plus times whose nearest fix has no altitude. \\
\texttt{z\_derivative\_reconstructed} & Derivative windows touched by altitude reconstruction. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
The flags identify grid times or derivative windows touched by reconstruction;
numerical completeness alone does not establish accuracy. The altitude-gap
bound concerns the separation of finite source readings, not the duration of
a consecutive run of flags on the new grid. With irregular source timing,
nearest-fix flagging can merge adjacent reconstructed portions into a longer
flagged run even when every finite-altitude bracket satisfies the bound.

\paragraph{Which segments are retained.}
A segment must span at least \SI{\PreprocMinSegmentS}{\second}, have at most
\SI{\PreprocMaxMissingPct}{\percent} of its grid times flagged as
\texttt{interpolated}, and yield finite values in all three coordinates.
The temporal-coverage fraction and the altitude-gap bound are separate criteria;
the latter is applied before interpolation.
A rejected segment leaves the other segments available; the parent's flight-level
cuts are not reapplied.

Segments retain the flight's spatial origin and elapsed time. Time-lagged displacement
pairs and analysis windows are formed within segments. Splitting therefore changes the
available coverage and can truncate flight phases; it must be accounted for when
comparing observables. Figure~\ref{fig:gaps} describes gaps in the raw archive;
observables that depend on the recording interval, such as
fix-to-fix turning angles, require a common temporal scale or a comparison within
cadence groups.

\subsection{Smoothing and differentiation}
\label{sec:savgol}

Position errors are amplified by differentiation. We estimate position, velocity and
acceleration from the same local polynomial, fitted independently to $E$, $N$ and $z$
within each retained segment. For $w=2m+1$ equally spaced samples around fix $i$, the
Savitzky--Golay fit~\cite{savitzky1964} is
\begin{align}
 P_i(u)&=\sum_{k=0}^{p}a_k(i)u^k,\qquad u=-m,\ldots,m,\label{eq:savgol-poly}\\
 \mathbf a(i)&=\underset{\mathbf a}{\arg\min}\,
       \sum_{u=-m}^{m}[x_{i+u}-P_i(u)]^2.\label{eq:savgol-lsq}
\end{align}
Here $u$ is a sample offset. The physical quantities at the window centre are
\begin{equation}
 \widehat x_i=a_0(i),\qquad
 \widehat v_i=\frac{a_1(i)}{\Delta t},\qquad
 \widehat a_i=\frac{2a_2(i)}{\Delta t^2}.
 \label{eq:savgol-physical}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The weights.}
With $V_{u,k}=u^k$ and $\mathbf x_i=(x_{i-m},\ldots,x_{i+m})^\top$, the mathematical
least-squares solution is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf a(i)=(V^\top V)^{-1}V^\top\mathbf x_i.
 \label{eq:savgol-solution}
\end{equation}
The inverse is a formula for the weights, not a recommendation to form a matrix inverse
numerically. The implementation uses SciPy's Savitzky--Golay coefficients. For fixed
$(w,p)$, row $k$ defines weights $c_k(u)$ with
\begin{equation}
 a_k(i)=\sum_{u=-m}^{m}c_k(u)x_{i+u}.
 \label{eq:savgol-convolution}
\end{equation}
This forward-offset convention is a correlation; convolution uses the reversed weights.
For an odd derivative the reversal changes their signs. Table~\ref{tab:savgol-kernels}
gives the forward-offset weights, before conversion to seconds.

The order $p=\PreprocSavgolOrder$ allows nonzero acceleration and locally varying
acceleration. A quadratic already estimates velocity and acceleration; a cubic is a
modelling choice, not a mathematical necessity. On a symmetric window, adding a cubic
term changes the first-derivative weights but leaves the value and second-derivative
weights of the quadratic fit unchanged.

\paragraph{Window length and cadence.}
A raw spectral diagnostic suggests a change in slope around \SI{0.2}{\hertz}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:savgol-spectrum}). It motivates the working timescale
\begin{equation}
 \tau_c=1/f_c=\SI{\PreprocSavgolTauCS}{\second},\qquad
 w=\max\!\left(\left\lceil\frac{\tau_c}{\Delta t}\right\rceil_{\mathrm{odd}},
               \PreprocSavgolMinWindow\right).
 \label{eq:savgol-window}
\end{equation}
\label{eq:savgol-tauc}
Horizontal and vertical timescales are separate configuration entries, currently equal.
The code converts this timescale to a \emph{number of samples}: the distance in time
between the first and last sample of a window is $(w-1)\Delta t$. Thus $w=5$ spans
\SI{4}{\second} at a \SI{1}{\second} cadence, and \SI{20}{\second} at a
\SI{5}{\second} cadence. Slower recording therefore changes the physical smoothing
scale and can remove motion that faster loggers resolve. Comparisons of short-time
observables must control for cadence.

The spectral knee is neither a fitted receiver-noise model nor the cutoff frequency
of this filter. Rounding a field to
steps of size $q$ has variance $q^2/12$ only under a uniform-error approximation;
a flat quantization spectrum additionally requires temporal independence. Similar
high-frequency spectra do not establish those assumptions or identify the source of
the observed knee.

\paragraph{Frequency response.}
In the segment interior, a Fourier mode
$x_i=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}fi\Delta t}$ is multiplied by

\begin{equation}
 G(f)=\sum_{u=-m}^{m}c_0(u)e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}fu\Delta t},\qquad
 S_{\widehat x}(f)=|G(f)|^2 S_x(f).
 \label{eq:savgol-response}
\end{equation}
The response depends on frequency and cadence through
\begin{equation}
 \nu=f\Delta t\in[0,\tfrac12],
 \label{eq:savgol-nu}
\end{equation}
in cycles per sample, with Nyquist at $\nu=1/2$. Thus the same $G(\nu)$ applies to
every cadence: $\nu=0.2$ corresponds to \SI{0.2}{\hertz} at
$\Delta t=\SI{1}{\second}$ and \SI{0.04}{\hertz} at
$\Delta t=\SI{5}{\second}$.

Symmetric weights give a real response,
$G(\nu)=c_0(0)+2\sum_{u=1}^{m}c_0(u)\cos(2\pi\nu u)$.
The phase is unchanged where $G>0$ and reversed by $\pi$ where $G<0$.
For the five-sample cubic fit (Table~\ref{tab:savgol-kernels}),

\begin{equation}
 G(\nu)=\frac{17+24\cos(2\pi\nu)-6\cos(4\pi\nu)}{35}.
 \label{eq:savgol-response-closed}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:savgol-response} shows the resulting power transmission.
The filter preserves constants, $G(0)=1$, and transmits half the power near
$\nu=0.24$. It vanishes at
$\nu_0=\arccos(1-\sqrt{35/12})/(2\pi)\approx0.375$, then rises to
$|G(1/2)|^2=(13/35)^2\approx0.14$. High frequencies are therefore attenuated
unevenly by this short kernel.

\paragraph{Effect on the MSD.}
Write one recorded coordinate as $\widetilde x_i=x_i+\varepsilon_i$.
For zero-mean errors independent of the true motion and of one another, with
$\mathbb E[\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j]=\sigma^2\delta_{ij}$, the one-coordinate
MSD at lag $\ell\ge1$ is

\begin{equation}
 \mathbb E\bigl[(\widetilde x_{i+\ell}-\widetilde x_i)^2\bigr]
   =\mathbb E\bigl[(x_{i+\ell}-x_i)^2\bigr]
    +\underbrace{\mathbb E\bigl[(\varepsilon_{i+\ell}-\varepsilon_i)^2\bigr]}_{2\sigma^2}.
 \label{eq:msd-noise-raw}
\end{equation}
The constant noise contribution matters most at short lags, where the true
displacement is small.

Smoothing replaces $\varepsilon_i$ by the weighted average
$\eta_i=\sum_u c_0(u)\varepsilon_{i+u}$, with $c_0$ extended by zero outside
$|u|\le m$. Independence of the raw errors leaves only the terms that read the same
fix, so with the weight autocorrelation $R(\ell)=\sum_u c_0(u)c_0(u-\ell)$,
\begin{align}
 \mathbb E[\eta_i\eta_{i+\ell}]&=\sigma^2R(\ell),\\
 B(\ell)&=\mathbb E[(\eta_{i+\ell}-\eta_i)^2]=2\sigma^2\bigl[R(0)-R(\ell)\bigr],
 \label{eq:savgol-msd-noise}
\end{align}
For $|\ell|\ge w$, the two windows share no raw errors and $R(\ell)=0$.

For $w=5$, $p=3$ the weights of Table~\ref{tab:savgol-kernels} give
\begin{equation}
 35^2R(\ell)=595,\;336,\;42,\;-72,\;9,\;0\qquad(\ell=0,\ldots,5),
 \label{eq:savgol-msd-values}
\end{equation}
Thus $B(\ell)/\sigma^2\approx0.42,\,0.90,\,1.09,\,0.96$ at
$\ell=1,\ldots,4$, settling at $34/35\approx0.97$ for $\ell\ge5$.
The filter roughly halves the noise contribution beyond its window but makes it
lag dependent inside, with an overshoot at $\ell=3$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:savgol-response}).
A horizontal MSD adds both coordinate contributions.

This calculation assumes independent errors; it does not calibrate GNSS noise.
The filter also changes the true motion unless each window follows a polynomial
of degree at most $p$. A Hurst exponent alone cannot bound that change.

\paragraph{Edges and validation.}
At either end of a segment, the filter fits the nearest full window and evaluates that
polynomial inside its fitted interval. These are off-centre evaluations, not
extrapolation beyond the observations. The first and last $m$ samples carry an
\texttt{edge} flag because their weights and error variance differ from the interior.
The filter can overshoot despite earlier cleaning, so speed bounds are not guaranteed
by smoothing.

The kernel identities and derivative units are checked in automated tests. Archive-level
validation remains separate: after rebuilding the cleaned archive, compare the MSD and
phase features at neighbouring odd window lengths, within each cadence class, and with
edge and reconstructed samples excluded. A flat residual spectrum is not a sufficient
criterion: even white input noise has residual PSD $|1-G(f)|^2S_\varepsilon(f)$.
Until these comparisons are reported, the adopted window is a working choice rather
than a certified optimum.

\subsection{Notation}
\label{sec:notation}
The coordinates and their derivatives are
\begin{align*}
 \mathbf r(t)&=(E(t),N(t)), & z(t)&=h_{\mathrm{GNSS}}(t),\\
 \mathbf v(t)&=\dot{\mathbf r}(t), & v_z(t)&=\dot z(t),\\
 v_{xy}(t)&=|\mathbf v(t)|, &
 \Delta\mathbf r(t_0,\tau)&=\mathbf r(t_0+\tau)-\mathbf r(t_0).
\end{align*}
The first trimmed fix sets $t=0$ and the horizontal origin; smoothing can shift
its position slightly. Altitude keeps its logged value. Every segment retains the
parent flight's origin and clock. A bare \emph{position} denotes the horizontal
vector $\mathbf r$; three-dimensional position is stated explicitly.

\begin{revblock}
\paragraph{The analysis ensemble.}
\label{par:ensemble}
The analysis ensemble comprises the retained segments written to
\path{fixes.parquet}, with one row per grid time carrying $(E,N,z)$ and their
first two derivatives. A flight must pass stages 1--4 and retain at least one
segment after resampling and smoothing. Segment origins and clocks remain those
of the first trimmed fix of the parent flight.

Transport and phase estimators impose their own cadence, lag-support and quality-mask
requirements, so their contributing populations can differ. The file schema and
estimator inputs are documented in \path{docs/guide/data-on-disk.md}.

\end{revblock}

\section{Trajectories for the transport analysis}

The retained trajectories have a uniform grid within each segment, local horizontal
coordinates and explicit reconstruction flags. Phase features can exclude flagged
vertical support, although plausible unflagged defects remain possible.
Geography, cadence and flight duration also vary across the archive.

Chapter~\ref{ch:global} defines each transport observable together with its
weights, lag support and contributing population.
