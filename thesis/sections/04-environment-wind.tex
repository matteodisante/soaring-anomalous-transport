\subsubsection{An independent coastal wind reference}
\label{sec:independent-coastal-wind}

The relevant vertical reference for the Channel Coast is the altitude of the
flights contributing to the \SI{10000}{\second} PCA. The cleaned GNSS altitude
$z$ is interpolated onto the same \SI{10}{\second} segment grids as the horizontal
coordinates. Trapezoidal integration gives a mean altitude along each of the
\num{\StatWindWindows} nonoverlapping displacement windows; averaging those
means with equal window weights gives \SI{\StatWindHeightM}{\metre}. The
\num{\StatWindFlights} flights and their segment boundaries are exactly those
of the PCA. Giving each flight equal weight instead gives
\SI{\StatWindHeightFlightM}{\metre}; the middle half of window-mean altitudes
lies between \SI{\StatWindHeightLowM}{\metre} and
\SI{\StatWindHeightHighM}{\metre}. These are absolute recorded altitude proxies,
not take-off altitude or height above the terrain. The receiver datums have
not been harmonised (Section~\ref{sec:enu}); the comparison interprets them
approximately as altitude above mean sea level, with limited physical precision.

ERA5 hourly pressure-level wind and geopotential \cite{era5_pressure} supply
an independent reference at that altitude. A pinned snapshot of the public
Earthmover archive \cite{earthmover_era5} provides 2016--2025 values at the same
nine $0.25^\circ$ cells used for the regional surface-wind reference. These
cells are nearest to the centres of a regular $3\times3$ partition of the
Channel Coast take-off box. The extracted levels are 1000, 925, 850 and
\SI{700}{\hecto\pascal}. For each cell and hour, geopotential $\Phi$ gives
geopotential height $H=\Phi/g_0$ and approximate geometric height
\cite{ecmwf_geopotential_height}
\begin{equation}
 h=\frac{R_\oplus H}{R_\oplus-H},\qquad
 g_0=\SI[group-digits=false]{9.80665}{\metre\per\second\squared},\quad
 R_\oplus=\SI{6371000}{\metre}.
 \label{eq:wind-level-height}
\end{equation}
The east and north wind components are linearly interpolated between the two
hourly level heights bracketing \SI{\StatWindHeightM}{\metre}. Both bounds
must be above the model terrain. All selected profiles satisfy these conditions;
no below-ground bound or vertical extrapolation enters the averages. A fixed
pressure level is therefore not assigned a fixed altitude.

The main comparison separates \emph{all hours throughout the year} from
\emph{09--17 UTC inclusive throughout the year}. April--September, with the
same two hourly selections, is a separate seasonal sensitivity. The ERA5 winds
at 10 and \SI{100}{\metre} above ground, retrieved through Open-Meteo
\cite{era5_hourly,open_meteo_historical}, remain lower-level controls. Their
meteorological \emph{from} directions $\phi$, clockwise from north, are converted
using $u=-s\sin\phi$, $v=-s\cos\phi$. All comparisons average the velocity
components over the selected hours and weight cells by
$\cos(\mathrm{latitude})$ to approximate represented area. The resulting
\emph{towards} angle,
\begin{equation}
 \theta_w=\operatorname{atan2}(\langle v\rangle,\langle u\rangle),
 \label{eq:regional-wind-axis}
\end{equation}
is counterclockwise from east and reduced modulo $180^\circ$ to compare with
the undirected PCA axis. Neither height interpolation nor temporal averaging
acts directly on degree angles.

Here $\rho_w=\|\langle\mathbf w\rangle\|/\langle\|\mathbf w\|\rangle$
measures directional cancellation, not confidence in an angle or the fraction
of winds near it. The reference PCA angle is
\SI{\StatWindFlightAngle}{\degree}.

At flight altitude the annual all-hour wind axis is
\SI{\StatWindHeightAllAngle}{\degree}, separated from the flight axis by
\SI{\StatWindHeightAllDifference}{\degree}. Restricting the same year-round
population to 09--17 UTC gives \SI{\StatWindHeightDayAngle}{\degree} and a
\SI{\StatWindHeightDayDifference}{\degree} separation. Thus the wind reference
and displacement spread share a broadly eastward orientation, with an estimated
offset of more than ten degrees. The warm-season selections retain
separations of about 10--14 degrees. At this altitude the temporal checks
therefore change the comparison less than at 10 and \SI{100}{\metre} AGL,
where the annual near alignment gives way to a larger warm-daytime offset
(Figure~\ref{fig:independent-coastal-wind}). Surface alignment cannot simply
be transferred to the altitude at which these flights take place.

A height sensitivity evaluates the two quartiles of window-mean altitude and
the equal-flight mean. At the lower quartile, some hours have no above-ground
pressure level below the target. The comparison therefore excludes
\num{\StatWindSensitivityExcludedHours} of the \num{87672} hours, about 1\%,
from \emph{every} tested height and cell, and recomputes the mean-height
reference on those same hours. The main estimates above retain all their
selected hours. On common support, the quartile-height annual axes span
\SI{\StatWindSensitivityAllLowAngle}{\degree}--\SI{\StatWindSensitivityAllHighAngle}{\degree}
for all hours and
\SI{\StatWindSensitivityDayLowAngle}{\degree}--\SI{\StatWindSensitivityDayHighAngle}{\degree}
for 09--17 UTC. Equal-flight altitude weighting changes the corresponding
mean-height axes by less than \SI{0.3}{\degree}; restricting the main altitude
to common support changes its annual axes by less than \SI{0.1}{\degree}.
These checks preserve the annual offset. They are sensitivity ranges, not
confidence intervals or a calibration of the GNSS datum.

The comparison remains descriptive. The wind climatology is not matched to
flight dates, positions or instantaneous altitudes, and a take-off box need
not contain whole flight routes. Moreover, the wind reference is a mean
velocity while the PCA describes \emph{centred} displacement covariance. A
common constant additive wind shifts every displacement at fixed lag by
$\tau\mathbf w$ and disappears under centring; variable winds, route choices
and flight selection can still affect the covariance. The angular resemblance
therefore does not establish a wind-driven mechanism or resistance to wind by
one equipment group. The useful next question is whether winds matched to
the observed flight conditions explain the remaining offset and covariance
orientation.

The complete selected hourly pressure-level observations are preserved
losslessly, with coordinates, timestamps, surface geopotential, source snapshot
and hashes, in
\path{revisions/channel-wind-flight-altitude-2026-09-13/}. The archive occupies
about 32.22 MB compressed; the earlier 10/100-m inputs occupy another 6.33 MB.
Both have SSD copies and offline reuse instructions. Only the regional extract
used here is stored. Keeping its hourly resolution supports vertical
interpolation before averaging, the directional distributions and separate
time selections at modest storage cost.
