# Chapter 3

## 1. What the chapter establishes

On the unsegmented trajectory, over 60–2000 s, cross-country soaring is a monofractal, non-Gaussian, directionally persistent motion with an effective filtered-variation exponent α₂ = 2.02 (paragliders) and 1.94 (hang gliders), a velocity memory that is small by 60 s and non-integrable, and no distinguished leg duration — it is not the Lévy walk the thesis was framed around, and no displacement statistic measured from take-off supplies any of it.

## 2. The argument arc

The obvious observable is measured first and fails as a source of exponents: the ensemble average about take-off departs from its own fitted power law by 22 to 51 times the standard error on each lag, its local slope arches above the ballistic value, and the arch sits where the population leaves its launch area — so what it yields is a timescale, and the same displacement averaged over starting times inside a record shows no such feature, which is what localises the failure to the common origin rather than to the observable. The section closes on the three contaminants of any displacement statistic here — the origin, the flown course, the heterogeneous population — and those three are the specification the filtered variation meets, without estimating anything, and the order scan reads the course off directly: removing it *raises* the exponent, because the archive's closed triangles suppress displacement. What that number is worth is then settled in one place — the resampling unit measured rather than assumed, a systematic term fifty times the sampling one, four stratifications that move the exponent by at most a tenth where the same strata moved the ensemble curve by 40–62 %, and a refusal to count regimes on 2 effective degrees of freedom. A third route, using neither a moment nor an origin, brackets the published H ≈ 0.88 and simultaneously shows the scaling form to be only approximate. The chapter then leaves the second moment: the moment spectrum is straight and no run length is distinguished, so the framing model is refused twice over; the propagator is nevertheless not Gaussian, and fourfold more so for hang gliders; and the memory, non-integrable, is what reconciles a correlation already small at 60 s with increments still correlated at 2000 s. It closes on two named gaps and on what the segmentation is obliged to reproduce.

## 3. The section list

**Chapter opening — no heading, ~1 page.** What an unsegmented measurement can fix (a class of transport) and cannot (the flight behaviour producing it). The analysis ensemble of Sec. 2.9 quoted through macros: 155 788 paraglider and 6 132 hang-glider flights, their segment counts, and the segment rule (computed within a retained segment, never across the gap that ends one). The two constraints Sec. 2.8 imposes: the components are anisotropic, so the marginals are never pooled; wing class and orographic group are incompatible on the ensemble MSD by 40–62 %, season is not, so the disciplines are never pooled and the strata are reported. **3.1 Displacement from take-off measures the launch geometry — ~5 pages.**

Ensemble MSD: rms departure from its own fitted power law 15.2 % and 31.2 %, against 22 to 51 times the standard error of each lag's own mean (the stored 10–90 band and count), reported as the evidence, with the sign-run count kept as a description of the arrangement and explicitly not as a test. The shape: local slope 1.31 → 2.21 → back, with the algebra that no positive superposition of exponents at or below 2 can give a local slope above 2, so the ballistic component switches on at a finite time; and the local-slope estimator's own window scatter, 0.017 against a point-to-point variation of 0.052, which is the quantitative statement that the arch is structure. Three controls, each measured: native-cadence classes (where the population cannot drift), fixed-duration cohorts (where it cannot be selected, against 1.11 on a synthetic ensemble in which only speed correlates with duration), and the mean vector's share of the mean square, which says the flights share a starting point and not a heading. The crossover measured, not fitted: first crossing of 5 km, median departure 1725 s and 1530 s against slope peaks at 1504 s and 1334 s. Then the time average over starting times inside a segment: α_TA = 1.732 and 1.688, residual four to eight times smaller, local slope wandering over a third the range — an effective exponent, and **no arch**, which is what identifies the crossover as origin-borne. The EA–TA gap reported and refused as evidence of ageing, in three sentences. One plain sentence that the quantity Chapter 1 defines the question with does not deliver an exponent on this archive. Closes on the three contaminants — origin, flown course, population — as the specification of §3.2. Figure: `msd.pdf`, four panels. — **Why here:** the failure is a measurement with its own positive yield and it generates everything after it, so it is first and it is titled by what it found rather than by what it lost; keeping both averages here is the ruling against by-observable's split, because the comparison *is* the diagnosis and the later of two sections is the wrong place to argue the earlier one's claim.

**3.2 The filtered variation — ~4 pages.**
The estimator and its measurement together. Why the drift is filtered and never subtracted, in one clause and one number (1.35 returned on a synthetic α = 1.50, with no safe lag range). A finite difference of order *p* annihilates every polynomial of degree *p*−1 identically, so V_p(Δ) = ⟨|A_p **r**(Δ)|²⟩ ∼ Δ^{2H} at every order and no drift is estimated, so none can be estimated badly. The fitted range stated rather than discovered, 60–2000 s, both ends argued. The order scan as the measurement: α₁ = 1.75/1.70, α₂ = 2.02/1.94, α₃ = 2.04/1.97, orders 2 and 3 agreeing to 0.02. Removing the course raises the exponent, the sign opposite to a residual drift's, confirmed directly by the closed-against-open task gap at order 1 (growing to +0.28 on a wider range) against order 2 (which does not move). α₁ reproducing α_TA of §3.1 across two implementations, labelled a check on the code and not on the physics. And, immediately, what a value near 2 does *not* mean: the same estimator returns 3.02 on a persistent walk of correlation time 3000 s and identically zero on a straight line, so 2 says persistent and still rough, not smooth. Figure: `transport.pdf`, plus a panel setting the local slope of V₂ against the local slope of the ensemble MSD on the same axes. — **Why here:** the replacement can only be introduced once the thing it replaces has failed on its own terms, and the α ≈ 2 calibration sits with the estimator that produced it rather than in the verdict twenty pages on, because H ≥ 1 is a red flag the reader raises the moment the number appears.

**3.3 The uncertainty on the exponent — ~4.5 pages.**
Three parts, one question: what the number is worth. *(a)* The denominator, measured: intraclass correlation of the per-flight variation at four levels — 0.00 flight, 0.57 day-and-site, 0.15 day, 0.29 pilot — so the resampling unit is the day and site, and the archive's cluster count. The two errors reported separately: day-and-site bootstrap 0.001 against range dependence 0.104, a factor of fifty; and why the least-squares error on the slope measures a different quantity, which is the retrospective justification for the bare figures of §3.1. What sets the window at each end, both from the system: the smoothing scale of the slowest retained logger below, the declared task above, whose closed-against-open separation in the local slope grows from 0.03 at 60 s to 1.13 at 12 000 s. *(b)* Four stratifications, each a row selection on the stored per-flight curves: native cadence 0.06 across a factor of ten in the smoothing scale, wing class, season, and the declared task at 0.10, the largest — against the 40–62 % by which the same strata moved the ensemble MSD, since a mixture of populations with different speeds and courses is a mixture of trends and a trend is what the second difference removes. **And the sentence the current chapter cannot write:** the task stratum (0.10) and the range dependence (0.104) are the same size because they are plausibly the same effect, the scoring rule governing the long end of the window; they are one term, not two to be added. *(c)* How many regimes the range supports: breakpoint selection returns 1 and 2, the same procedure on surrogates that are one power law plus the measured sampling noise returns a break 16 % and 34 % of the time, and 22 lags carry no more than 2.0 effective degrees of freedom because every lag averages the same flights. Neither count is established. — **Why here:** 0.06 is unreadable until 0.104 exists, which is why the systematic precedes the strata inside one section rather than after them; and the regime count is not a fifth null but the same question at the limit — how many exponents the range supports — so it closes the section rather than trailing four robustness rows.

**3.4 The exponent from the increment quantiles — ~4 pages.**
Everything above is a second moment; this is neither a moment nor a common origin. First the negative that was tried for exactly this role: first-passage times, 2.46 naively (impossible for stationary increments), 1.95 and 2.03 once non-arrival is treated as censored, then 1.51 to 2.03 across sub-ranges of radius — five times the uncertainty it was meant to check, and the reason is §3.1's reason transposed, the launch geometry carried into the space domain. Then the estimator that avoids both: P(x,Δ) = Δ^{−H} F(x/Δ^H), so every quantile of |Δx| grows as Δ^H; a quantile is an order statistic, fixed by the middle of the distribution, unmoved by a heavy tail. H ≈ 0.90, from the streaming histograms, moving by 0.035 across cadences spanning a factor of ten in the smoothing scale. The three-route table: 0.876/0.852 from the plain increment, ≈0.90/0.885 from the bulk, 1.009/0.972 from the second-order variation, set against the published H ≈ 0.88 — reproduced by the estimator that matches it, with the gap identified as the flown course and its sign predicted by §3.2. Then the cost, stated in the same section: the four quantiles do not share a slope, H falling from 0.926 at the lower quartile and median to 0.872 at the upper quartile and ninetieth percentile, in both disciplines, on both components, at every cadence; spread 0.077 and 0.098 against 0.004 on an exactly self-similar process measured the same way with less data, and the collapse showing the same in dex of scatter. The anisotropy reaches the exponent: H_E against H_N, 0.018 for paragliders and more for hang gliders. Figure: `propagator.pdf`. — **Why here:** the route is only interesting as a third leg, so it follows the two moments it brackets; a route is never separated from its cost, so the failure of the scaling form stays in the section that assumed it; and the withdrawn first-passage time is housed with the role it was auditioning for rather than beside the ensemble MSD, because it was tried *after* α₂ existed and its censored values are calibrated against α₂ ± 0.10.

**3.5 The archive is not a Lévy walk — ~2.5 pages.**
Two independent routes to one refusal. The moment spectrum: ⟨|Δ**r**|^q⟩ ∼ Δ^{qν(q)}, straight through the origin for a monofractal, bilinear for a Lévy walk with a knee at its tail index and a right branch of slope one. Over the fitted range and q from q_min to q_max, ν varies by 0.019 and 0.009, and the departure from a line through the origin is quoted against **both** references separately, no longer conflated: about 0.084 for a Lévy walk of index 1.5 on this grid, and 0.02 for the gate at which a knee is declared — a margin of an order of magnitude against the knee, a factor of two against the gate. Estimability: the share of each moment carried by the largest one per cent of its increments, against the half above which a moment is one flight. Then the reconciliation this chapter owes and none of its predecessors housed: §3.4 says the collapse is not exact and this section says every moment scales with one exponent, and the two are compatible because a moment integrates over the whole distribution while a quantile fit reads it at four fixed probability levels — the paragraph states the bound and, if the full propagator pass does not support it, states that instead. Second route, the persistence runs: non-overlapping stretches whose sinuosity stays under a threshold, their durations the geometric counterpart of a waiting time, tail index moving from 4.41 to 2.24 as the threshold is scanned over 1.05, 1.15, 1.30. No distinguished leg duration for a Lévy description to rest on, and a prediction about Chapter 4. Figure: panels (a), (b), (e), (f) of the split `shape.pdf`. — **Why here:** the model the thesis was framed around is refused in one section whose heading says so, because a verdict established twice and stated nowhere is a verdict a reader meets in halves; it follows §3.4 so that straightness is read against an already-declared qualification rather than before it.

**3.6 The propagator is not Gaussian — ~1.5 pages.**
The shape, from the fourth and second moments §3.5 has already accumulated: the non-Gaussian parameter (written without the subscript 2, declared here, since α₂ is taken). +0.04 for paragliders and +0.18 for hang gliders over 60–2000 s, flat across the range, against a fractional Brownian motion whose 0.02 is finite-sample bias and collapses to within a hundredth of zero at sixteen times the data, and 0.55 for a Lévy walk of index 1.5 under the same estimator. Both non-Gaussian, neither near Lévy, and the fourfold difference between the disciplines reported and not averaged: whatever makes these increments heavy acts about four times more strongly on the smaller, slower wing. The climb beyond the fitted range reported and not quoted, with the reason. Closing sentence: monofractal is not the same as Gaussian — one exponent for every moment says how the distribution scales and nothing about its shape. Figure: panel (c). — **Why here:** a separate heading from §3.5 on purpose, because the conflation of a straight spectrum with a Gaussian propagator is an error this project published once and withdrew on verification, and two questions out of one set of moments must be measured, attacked and closed under two headings.

**3.7 The memory of the heading — ~3 pages.**
C(τ) positive throughout, no sign change anywhere on the grid, where a sign change would be the anti-persistence a circling wing produces — so at the ensemble level the circling does not survive the average, which is a constraint on Chapter 4. Below 1/e within the first two lags of a grid whose floor is the smoothing scale rather than a property of the flying. The objection stated plainly — a memory spent by 60 s and increments correlated at 2000 s cannot both hold — and the resolution as the section's result: Green–Kubo turns on integrability, not on size, and a tail C(τ) ∼ τ^{−γ} with γ < 1 sustains Δ^{2−γ} however small C already is. Measured γ = 0.61 and 0.57, non-integrable in both. The comparison one-sided by construction (per-flight mean removal steepens the tail; the integral's first decade lies below the grid), so 2 − γ is a floor: 1.39 and 1.43, cleared by α₁ = 1.75 and 1.70. Figure: panel (d). — **Why here:** it is the only observable that could have contradicted the exponent, so it comes after the exponent is fixed; it is four sections rather than one from α₁ and pays for that by restating the two numbers it compares, which is the price of not fragmenting the shape results.

**3.8 Observables defined and not measured — ~0.75 page.**
Two entries, ranked, each with a definition in one line, what it would settle, and why it is not here. The return probability P₀(t) = P(**0**,t), decaying as t^{−dH}: it gives H from the peak of the propagator where §3.4 gives it from four fixed probability levels — a different functional of the same F — and it falls out of histograms the propagator pass already holds, so it is the cheapest remaining check on the headline. The turning-angle distribution: the microscopic origin of the persistence §3.7 measures and §3.2 reads as an exponent, whose segment-level counterpart Chapter 4's model requires. One closing sentence: first-passage times are absent from this list because they were measured and withdrawn, in §3.4. — **Why here:** a list of gaps read before the measurements is a promise and read after them is an inventory, which is why there is no front table for it to contradict.

**3.9 What this establishes, and what the segmentation receives — ~2.5 pages.**
The headline with its full scope in one paragraph. The class of process assembled from §3.4–§3.7 in one paragraph: monofractal, non-Gaussian and fourfold more so for hang gliders, with a non-integrable memory and no distinguished leg duration — neither a Lévy walk nor a Gaussian one. The limits in one paragraph: 1.5 decades, smoothed below and task-governed above, the scaling form itself only approximate, "effective exponent over 1.5 decades" and "the transport is characterised by α = 2.02" named as different statements with only the first made, and the cutoff physical rather than methodological because a soaring day ends. A short register of what was withdrawn and what each withdrawal bought: the ensemble MSD as a source of exponents (it bought the departure timescale), the first-passage time, the runs statistic as a significance, the Gaussian reading of a straight spectrum. Then the hand-over as numbered constraints: (i) any statistic measured from take-off inherits the launch geometry, and 1725 s / 1530 s is where that stops governing; (ii) a phase decomposition, recombined, must return α₂ = 2.02/1.94 over 60–2000 s, and a disagreement is evidence about the segmentation — stated as additivity of the estimator, not as a property of the transport, so it does not contradict the limits paragraph; (iii) a climb-glide threshold will move the phase statistics as the sinuosity threshold moved β, so it must be scanned and the movement reported; (iv) the components are analysed separately; (v) the fourfold discipline gap in non-Gaussianity is a fact any mechanism must explain; (vi) the ergodicity question is handed on in the two forms that need no power law — the exponent's drift with time already flown, and the ergodicity-breaking parameter of the CTRW appendix. — **Why here:** the only section permitted to speak across observables, which is what lets the seven measurement sections stay self-contained, and the hand-over is inherently cross-observable because Chapter 4 inherits a class of transport plus a list of obligations, not an observable.

## 6. Where the new propagator result goes

**§3.4, in two parts, in that order.** Part one is the exponent: H ≈ 0.90 from the quantiles of |Δx|, an order statistic with no moment and no origin, moving by 0.035 across cadences spanning a factor of ten in the smoothing scale; set beside 0.876/0.852 from the plain increment and 1.009/0.972 from the second-order variation, and against the published H ≈ 0.88, which the estimator that matches it reproduces with the gap identified as the flown course and its sign predicted in §3.2. The load-bearing claim is the *bracket* — three routes, two of them second moments in different frames and one no moment at all — not the third decimal, so a full pass that moves H by a hundredth changes nothing in the argument and the section is written that way.

**Part two is the cost, in the same section**: the four quantiles do not share a slope, H falling from 0.926 at the bulk to 0.872 at the flank, spread 0.077 and 0.098 against 0.004 on an exactly self-similar process measured the same way with less data. This is a qualification and not a refutation — 0.05 between bulk and flank is small beside the distance from Brownian — and it is the second independent reason, beside the range dependence of §3.3, why a single α quoted to three figures would claim more than the archive supports. It is quoted in that form in §3.9's limits paragraph.

**It then obliges one paragraph in §3.5**, and that paragraph is the chapter's sharpest internal tension: the quantiles say the collapse is not exact and the spectrum says every moment scales with one exponent. The reconciliation is that the two read the same collapse at different levels — a moment integrates over the whole distribution and a bulk-to-flank drift partly cancels in that average, whereas a quantile fit reads it at four fixed probability levels and does not. §3.5 states that with a number, computed from the stored histograms: the bulk-to-flank drift implies a ν(q) variation of a stated size, to be compared with the measured 0.019 and 0.009. If it does not account for it, that is a finding and §3.5 says so instead. Setting a tension up and leaving it to a summary is what three of the five candidate structures do, and it is not acceptable at this standard.

## 7. What this structure sacrifices

1. **The Lévy verdict still arrives at about page 21 of 29.** Only the heading — visible in the table of contents — and the chapter opening's third sentence protect a reader who came to this thesis for that question. A structure that promoted it would have to demote the exponent, and the exponent is what everything else is measured against.
2. **§3.1 withdraws one estimator and retains another under a heading that names only the withdrawal.** α_TA = 1.732/1.688 is quoted in a section titled about launch geometry and is used again in §3.2 as the implementation check, which risks reading as double counting. The alternative — a separate section for the time average — costs the ensemble-against-time-average comparison its home, and that comparison is the diagnosis.
3. **The propagator is dismembered.** Its exponent and its quantile spread are in §3.4, its non-Gaussian shape in §3.6, its anisotropy in §3.4; a reader who wants "the propagator" assembles it from two sections, and the histograms are touched three times while P₀(t) is read none of them.
4. **§3.3 is four and a half pages of epistemics with no new physics in it**, sitting between the chapter's two measurement sections. A reader in a hurry skips it, and it contains the chapter's best work — the measured resampling unit, the fifty-fold systematic, and the refusal to count regimes on 2 effective degrees of freedom.
5. **Nine sections, two of them under 1.5 pages, in a chapter that is still about a quarter of the document.** §3.6 and §3.8 are short by design and will read as thin unless each carries a closing verdict sentence that earns its heading.
6. **The α₂ collision survives.** The filter order and the non-Gaussian parameter are separated by three sections here rather than seventeen lines, which is an improvement, but the fix is still a rename declared in §3.6 and a reader may arrive with the literature's convention.
7. **The chronology disappears.** The quantile route was built last, after a verification pass, and the first-passage time was tried as a cross-check on α₂ once α₂ existed; this ordering presents them as designed steps. §3.4 must never imply otherwise, and the sentence "this was attempted afterwards, as a check" is awkward to write anywhere.
8. **One true statement is demoted.** That the ensemble MSD's failure and the filtered variation's success are the same fact seen twice — a mixture of populations is a mixture of trends, and a trend is what the second difference removes — appears as one sentence in §3.3(b) rather than as the chapter's spine.
9. **The 1.5-decade limit is split**: what sets the window is in §3.3, the verdict on it in §3.9, so neither half sits where a reader hunting for what is wrong with the chapter would look.

## 8. The order to write it in

1. **§3.2, the filtered variation.** The spine; every other section is positioned relative to it, and its content is settled and generated. Writing it first fixes the notation (A_p, V_p, α_p) that §3.1 must close on and §3.6 must avoid.
2. **§3.3, the uncertainty.** Immediately after, because the 0.10 ≈ 0.104 confrontation is new prose that no existing section contains and it may change what §3.2's table reports.
3. **§3.1, displacement from take-off.** Written third, not first, because its closing paragraph is the specification §3.2 meets and that specification is only exact once §3.2 exists. Most of its material exists; the work is compression to five pages and the merger of the two averages under one heading.
4. **§3.9, what this establishes.** Written fourth, in draft, so that the six hand-over constraints are fixed before the shape sections are written — three of them are what §3.5 and §3.7 are for, and a chapter feeding a plan should know what it is handing over before it writes the sections that produce it.
5. **§3.4, the increment quantiles.** Blocked on the full propagator pass. Write the first-passage paragraph and the three-route comparison now, from numbers already in hand; hold the quantile-spread figures until the pass lands.
6. **§3.5 and §3.6, the Lévy refusal and the propagator's shape.** After §3.4, because §3.5's reconciliation paragraph needs §3.4's spread. Split `shape.pdf` into two figures before writing either.
7. **§3.7, the memory.** Independent of everything after §3.2; write it whenever §3.2's α₁ is final.
8. **§3.8, the two gaps.** Half a page, written once §3.4 is final, because what P₀(t) adds beyond the quantiles is only statable then.
9. **The chapter opening.** Last, because its three declarative sentences are the chapter's findings and they must be quoted, not composed.
10. **C3, renumbered to mirror §3.1–§3.9**, and the generators for the typed numbers, run alongside from step 1 — the appendix transfers in item 4 above cannot happen until those numbers have macros.
