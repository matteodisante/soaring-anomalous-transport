## Findings corrected

1. **The archive TAMSD is an equal-segment average.** It had been described alongside
   flight/window weighting without stating its actual weights. A split flight contributes
   once per segment. The chapter states this and shows the discrete sum;
   figure counts and percentile bands explicitly distinguish flights from segments.
   Native-grid rounding means archive lags can differ from their displayed labels by
   half a native step. The exact 10 s grid belongs to the separate diagnostic subset.
2. **Regional kinematics uses raw second moments, not centred variances.** The source
   computes `<u_E^2>/<u_N^2>` at time since launch. Both mean motion and fluctuation
   anisotropy contribute. The chapter, figure caption and generator documentation now
   state this. Position derivatives do not separate terrain, wind and pilot decisions.
3. **The plotted VACF is a mean of normalised segment correlations.** It is not obtained
   by normalising one covariance pooled across flights. Added the finite-sample equation,
   the `n-k` pair count, segment centring, contributing-set definition and exact block
   support bound. Its relation to Green–Kubo still requires a stationary homogeneous
   velocity population; the uncentred archive MSD is not such a population by definition.
4. **FFT MSD had avoidable cancellation for small motion at large coordinate offsets.**
   Coordinates are translated before squaring and FFT correlation; zero lag is now
   exactly zero and negative floating-point round-off is clamped at zero. A direct-sum
   oracle at an offset of `1e12` verifies preservation of metre-scale displacement.
   The retained archive CSV was redrawn, not remeasured: the next full archive measurement
   incorporates this numerical fix.
5. **Cache reuse could misstate provenance.** It checked only the fixes file and then
   wrote current code hashes beside old measurements. The cache now records/checks source
   fixes, flight metadata, task labels, estimator fingerprint, lag/moment/velocity grids,
   region boxes, sampling parameters and seed. Changed estimators or inputs require a
   new measurement. Rendering and summary changes can reuse the same measured arrays.
   The bounded subset was remeasured; it still contains 877 paraglider and 430 hang-glider
   flights from the documented old cleaning snapshot. Manifest hashes and cache validation
   pass after regeneration.
6. **Several legacy docstrings contradicted the revised science.** Removed claims that
   archive EA/TA disagreement proves nonergodicity, anisotropy alone breaks radial scaling,
   the median is insensitive to arbitrary tail changes, a fitted knee is an independent
   estimate, or the local-slope scatter is a calibrated uncertainty. Historical broken-line
   spectrum preference is documented as a heuristic on correlated moment exponents.
7. **Notation and assumptions were incomplete in places.** Explicitly centred the Gaussian
   anisotropy counterexample, specified the whitening reference covariance and the
   finite-dimensional definition of self-similarity, derived the second-difference
   prefactor from adjacent increments, and explained why isotropic 2D radial moments share
   the exponent of a 1D projection. The circle drawing is a periodically continued path,
   so evaluating second differences beyond one period is mathematically defined.

## Deductions retained, with their limits

- Quantile scaling and radial/component Jacobians are correct under a joint common-scale
  law. Differing fitted quantile slopes can reflect shape evolution or changing sampled
  populations. PCA rotates coordinates without changing radii; whitening changes the
  metric and does not establish physical isotropy.
- Constant drift cancels exactly in a second difference. Recovering `H` from half its
  slope requires the stated stationary-increment finite-variance scaling assumptions.
  Smooth curved paths can instead give fourth-power second-difference growth.
- Mardia excess is centred and covariance-standardised. Zero excess is only a necessary
  Gaussian moment condition; pooled nonzero excess can arise from conditional mixtures.
- The standard renewal Lévy-walk spectrum is correct for `1 < beta < 2`; the displayed
  `beta=1.5` curve is illustrative, not a fitted or simulation-calibrated null.
- **No calibrated exclusion has been produced.** Homogeneous Brownian/fractional Brownian
  descriptions and the stated asymptotic Lévy-walk benchmark are descriptively challenged.
  Finite-persistence models, mixtures, truncated walks and conditional descriptions remain
  open. An unbounded Lévy flight is unsuitable as a literal finite-speed mechanism; this
  does not exclude all effective coarse models with jumps.
- The duration comparison does not isolate an observation-duration effect and does not
  demonstrate independence. Four-hour cohorts differ appreciably in amplitude.

## Primary sources checked

- Mandelbrot and Van Ness (1968), SIAM Review 10(4), 422–437, DOI
  [10.1137/1010093](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/abs/10.1137/1010093).
  Publisher metadata confirms title, authors and print year; the 2006 web publication
  date must not replace 1968. The stationary-increment scaling identity is also derived
  directly in the chapter rather than relying on an uncited prefactor.
- Mardia (1970), Biometrika 57(3), 519–530, DOI
  [10.1093/biomet/57.3.519](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/57/3/519/253220).
  Publisher metadata and abstract match the bibliography and scope of multivariate
  skewness/kurtosis. Full text was not accessible through the publisher; the stated
  Gaussian reference and scale-mixture identity were checked algebraically.
- Zaburdaev, Denisov and Klafter (2015), Reviews of Modern Physics 87, 483,
  [author manuscript and journal DOI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.5100).
  [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.5100), Sec. II.A.4, Eq. (35) and Fig. 5 verify
  the MSD regime and two-branch moment spectrum.
- Rebenshtok, Denisov, Hänggi and Barkai (2014), Physical Review E 90, 062135,
  [author manuscript and journal DOI](https://arxiv.org/abs/1408.4479).
  [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4479), Eqs. (72), (75), (76) verify the two
  moment branches. This is the PRE article, distinct from their 2014 PRL article.

No correction to those four bibliography entries was required. Metadata verification
is distinct from checking whether a citation supports a particular empirical deduction.
