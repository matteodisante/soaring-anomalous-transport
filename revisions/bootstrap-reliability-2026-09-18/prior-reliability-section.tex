\paragraph{Reliability of bootstrap uncertainty estimates.}
\label{sec:fixed-bootstrap-reliability}

Site--day grouping can miss dependence between sites and successive days. We
assess its effect by enlarging bootstrap blocks while keeping the main cohort
and equal-flight estimator fixed~\cite{cameron_miller2015}. Each block contains
all sites over $L=1,2,4,8,16,32,64$ consecutive calendar days. One-day blocks
allow dependence across sites; longer blocks also allow dependence across days.
For each duration, 1000 block-bootstrap replicates give standard errors of the
MSD at 100, 1000 and 10000 s and of $H$ fitted over 10--10000 s. Shifting block
boundaries and using two seeds checks partition sensitivity and Monte Carlo noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fixed-bootstrap-reliability}).

\begin{figure}[htbp]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/ch3_transport_bootstrap_reliability.pdf}
 \caption{Bootstrap standard errors relative to the site--day baseline (dashed
 line). Markers show medians across boundary offsets and two seeds; vertical bars
 span their minimum and maximum, not confidence intervals. The flight population
 and estimator are unchanged.}
 \label{fig:fixed-bootstrap-reliability}
\end{figure}

For paragliders, grouping all sites in 1-day blocks multiplies the standard error of
$H$ by \ChThreeParaBlockHurstShort; 64-day blocks give \ChThreeParaBlockHurstLong. At
64 days, the factors across the three MSD diagnostics span \ChThreeParaBlockMsdSpan.
There are \ChThreeParaBlockCount{} contributing blocks, with at most
\ChThreeParaBlockMaxPct\% of the cohort in one block.

For hang gliders, grouping all sites in 1-day blocks multiplies the standard error of
$H$ by \ChThreeHangBlockHurstShort; 64-day blocks give \ChThreeHangBlockHurstLong. At
64 days, the factors across the three MSD diagnostics span \ChThreeHangBlockMsdSpan.
There are \ChThreeHangBlockCount{} contributing blocks, with at most
\ChThreeHangBlockMaxPct\% of the cohort in one block.

To locate the dependence directly, we sum centred flight contributions within
each calendar day $d$ and examine their autocovariance:
\begin{equation}
 U_d(\tau)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal C_d}\bigl[m_f(\tau)-M_2(\tau)\bigr],
 \qquad
 \widehat\rho_\tau(h)=
 \frac{\sum_d U_d(\tau)U_{d+h}(\tau)}{\sum_d U_d(\tau)^2}.
 \label{eq:fixed-daily-dependence}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal C_d$ contains the cohort flights recorded on day $d$; empty dates
contribute zero. Summing rather than averaging within days preserves equal-flight
weighting. For $H$, we use its first-order per-flight contributions to the fitted
slope~\cite{wolff2004}. As a seasonal sensitivity check, we repeat after subtracting
the flight-weighted mean contribution for each calendar month, pooled across years.
This centring affects only the diagnostic.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{generated/ch3_transport_daily_dependence.pdf}
 \caption{Daily dependence for the MSD at 1000 s and fitted $H$. Solid curves use
 the original contributions; dashed curves subtract month-of-year means.
 Separations are actual calendar days. These are descriptive correlograms,
 without significance bands.}
 \label{fig:fixed-daily-dependence}
\end{figure}

For paragliders, the MSD at 1000 s gives $\widehat{\rho}(1)=\ChThreeParaDailyRho$
(\ChThreeParaDailyRhoCentred{} after monthly centring); the mean over 17--64 days is
\ChThreeParaDailyTail{} (\ChThreeParaDailyTailCentred).

For hang gliders, the MSD at 1000 s gives $\widehat{\rho}(1)=\ChThreeHangDailyRho$
(\ChThreeHangDailyRhoCentred{} after monthly centring); the mean over 17--64 days is
\ChThreeHangDailyTail{} (\ChThreeHangDailyTailCentred).

Monthly centring does not remove the MSD tail. The weaker long-separation
contributions for $H$ agree with its greater stability under wider blocks, but
neither check identifies a common decorrelation scale. We therefore retain the
site--day intervals as conditional baseline estimates. Persistent changes in
site, pilot or seasonal composition remain possible; the checks establish neither
independence nor coverage, and do not validate quantile or kurtosis intervals.
