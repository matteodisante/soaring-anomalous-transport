# Thermal planes in the viewer

Launch `uv run --group viewer soaring-viewer` and open **Thermal planes**.
The tab reads **one standalone file**, `thermal-planes.sqlite3`, in read-only
mode. It defaults to Vilpellet and the full saved date range. Date controls use
Europe/Paris local time (CET/CEST) and do not reset on cell, height, background,
segmentation or reload changes.
**Reload SSD data** only reopens that file: there is no preparation button and no
fallback to archive scanning or segmentation inside this view.

The default destination is `derived/viewer/thermal-planes/` in the first available
archive on the SSD. With the current configuration this is:

```
/Volumes/SSD_DISANTE/paragliders/ffvl_cfd_igc/derived/viewer/thermal-planes/
```

`thermal-planes.sqlite3` (format 2) contains the twelve selected cells, their ground references
and maxima, UTC visitor intervals, and compressed climb edges for both methods
and all dates. It works without opening the original IGC files, parquet tables or
models. The enriched file also contains **already interpolated intersections**
at every 10 m AGL and at each cell’s exact maximum. Slider steps of 10, 20, 50,
100 and 200 m select subsets of this same lattice. The viewer only reads, filters
and plots these saved points; it neither computes intersections nor segments
flights. Legacy snapshots without the lattice still support in-memory edge
interpolation until upgraded offline.

Preparation is a separate offline command:

```bash
uv run --group viewer python scripts/pipeline/prepare_thermal_planes.py --workers 4
```

The command resumes intermediate `thermal-cells.sqlite3` and
`thermal-climbs.sqlite3` products. It decodes each needed whole flight once per
method, sharing that result across cells. Only after verifying complete coverage
for both methods does it atomically replace the viewer's standalone file.
An interruption leaves the previous published file usable and retains completed
intermediate work. Use `--index-only` for just the census, or `--rebuild-index`
after modifying raw IGC files in place. `SOARING_VIEWER_CACHE_DIR` overrides the
destination. Re-run this command when inputs or segmentation settings change;
the viewer deliberately reads the published snapshot without inspecting inputs.

Select one of the three ranked cells in each of the four categories, enter **From** and **To** as calendar dates and
times in **Paris local time**, choose **This work (HMM)** or **Jérémie (Vilpellet)**, and click
**Load climb intersections**. The height slider then moves the horizontal plane
without rereading flights or repeating segmentation. The left map also accepts
clicks on the cell markers or their labelled callouts. Changing the time interval or segmentation hides
the previous result until the new selection is loaded.

## Cell population and ground reference

The scope matches the metropolitan France panel of thesis Figure 2.24, pooling
both available disciplines and using the retained, processed ensemble. The
geographic window is longitude −5.5 to 10 degrees, latitude 41 to 51.5 degrees.
The old 0.15-degree mesh is not used: the new partition is a fixed Lambert-93
(EPSG:2154) grid with edges at integer multiples of 5000 metres. These are metric
map squares; physical distances carry the small scale distortion of the map
projection. Existing thesis figures are not regenerated by this viewer feature.

Each cell counts every **distinct flight that crosses it**, including flights
whose launch is outside. Repeated visits count once. Supported edges are split
at grid boundaries, so an intervening cell is included even when neither recorded
endpoint lies inside it. No line is drawn across a preprocessing segment boundary
or a temporal gap.

The **ground reference** is computed independently: the median GNSS altitude of
the first raw, parseable fix of retained flights whose first raw position is
inside that cell, **before trimming**. Zero/missing altitudes and values outside
the preprocessing plausibility bounds are excluded. No barometric fallback or
post-trim origin altitude is substituted. The number of usable starts is shown.
Cells without such a reference cannot be candidates.

Before computing that median, a separate offline audit screens the **same first
raw fix**. An invalid GNSS declaration excludes that origin. The first fix is also
excluded when **two later valid fixes** both imply a horizontal speed above the
preprocessing limit (45 m/s for paragliders, 55 m/s for hang gliders), or both imply
a vertical speed above 10 m/s relative to the origin. The screen examines at most
20 parseable fixes, within 120 seconds, and at most five eligible witnesses.
Midnight is unwrapped; nonpositive time differences and missing later altitudes
are not witnesses. One anomalous later fix alone cannot reject a good origin.
Fewer than two witnesses are recorded as `unchecked` and retained. No replacement
altitude is taken from a later fix, and no trimming is used. Crossing counts and
climb geometries are unaffected. The separate `thermal-launch-quality.sqlite3`
allows the audit to resume without invalidating climb products.

These names are **bands of estimated starting altitude**, not a geomorphological
classification or a DEM lookup. A low valley can be labelled Plains. A median based
on very few starts is fragile even after the jump screen; the viewer displays the
number of supporting starts and highlights fewer than ten. Such cells remain
eligible, without an arbitrary minimum-population cut.

The median assigns the cell to Plains (<300 m), Hills (300 to <800 m), Low
mountains (800 to <1500 m), or High mountains (≥1500 m). The three busiest crossing cells
in each band are offered; ties use grid coordinates for deterministic ordering.
This uses the median rather than the majority-of-launch-bands rule in the old
thesis map. The rankings, launch medians and maximum heights use **all dates** and
do not change with the selected time interval or segmentation.

The AGL slider runs from zero to the highest supported trajectory altitude
**inside the cell**, minus its ground reference. Boundary intersections contribute
to this maximum too. It is not the maximum of entire flights outside the square,
nor the maximum restricted to climb-labelled fixes. AGL here means height above
one fixed launch median, not height above a terrain model at each horizontal point.

## Ranked map and shaded terrain

The dropdown and map callouts show category, within-category rank, distinct
crossing flights, and the median launch altitude in metres ASL. The cell summary
also reports the raw support and median before the separate origin screen, so
the effect of that policy is visible.
Map labels **P1–P3**, **H1–H3**, **L1–L3**, **M1–M3** use category colours and
separated callouts, which are also clickable. Here M denotes High mountains;
1 always means the busiest cell **within that category**, not across categories.
Neighbouring 5 km cells may be hard to distinguish at France scale; use the map
zoom controls for their exact footprints.

Both plots show a subdued [Esri World Hillshade](https://services.arcgisonline.com/arcgis/rest/services/Elevation/World_Hillshade/MapServer)
backdrop. The offline command downloads a France overview in EPSG:4326 and a
600 × 600 image per cell in EPSG:2154, retaining the returned bounds and source
metadata. The same Lambert-93 bounds locate the cell image and climb points.
All PNGs are embedded in `thermal-planes.sqlite3`; the viewer never requests tiles
or contacts the service. The **% background** control adjusts both backgrounds without
changing the selected data. Points use a contrasting colour and a white edge.

Source attribution: Esri, Vantor, Airbus DS, USGS, NGA, NASA, CGIAR, N Robinson,
NCEAS, NLS, OS, NMA, Geodatastyrelsen, Rijkswaterstaat, GSA, Geoland, FEMA, Intermap,
and the GIS user community. The hillshade is only a visual background: it does not
replace the GNSS ground reference or change the meaning of the AGL slider.

## Time and segmentation

UTC uses the IGC date header and the time of the first parseable B record. The
archived elapsed clock was reset by trimming; its `ground_phase_start_s` offset
is added back. Consequently, midnight rollovers and flights starting before the
requested interval can still contribute. Flights without a recoverable UTC
origin remain in the cell census and are explicitly reported as unavailable for
the calendar-time selection; catalog dates are not silently substituted.

Whole archived flights are decoded before any spatial or temporal cuts, using
the viewer's corresponding segmentation loader. Jérémie's existing eligibility
guards remain active. Missing models/configuration and entirely unclassified
flights are reported separately. Selecting one method never silently falls back
to the other.

Dots are linearly interpolated **intersections of climb-labelled trajectory
edges with the plane**, clipped to the square and requested UTC interval. Both
endpoints must belong to one continuous climb run. Upward and downward crossings
within that run are included; shared vertices are counted once. Horizontal
coplanar edges have no isolated intersection and are omitted. Several dots can
belong to one thermal: these are observed climb crossings, not an estimator of
thermal centres or a clustering result.

The census is invalidated by changes to archive paths, catalog, flight metadata,
fix tables or the altitude plausibility bounds. Each method's climb products are
also keyed by its model, configuration and decoding/geometry code. Changing one
method does not invalidate the other. A rebuilt census invalidates climb products
too, including their UTC origins. A new viewer session or a different time interval
does not invalidate anything. Missing products are prepared only by the offline command;
the viewer reports how many flights were read from the published file.

The complete census is published atomically. Every whole-flight climb product is
committed independently, so resuming preparation skips finished products. Original
IGC files, processed tables and segmentation models are never modified.


## Colour maps, aerial dates and daily comparison

**Colour map · IGN**, **Aerial photo · IGN**, **Shaded relief**, and **None** are
available for both maps. The IGN photographs are aerial orthophotos, not a live
satellite feed. Each 5 km square is saved at 4000 × 4000 pixels (1.25 m per pixel),
with its exact EPSG:2154 bounds; the France overview uses CRS:84. Zooming uses the
same saved image, without fetching tiles. Roof material or crop type should not
be inferred solely from colour, and the imagery is not contemporaneous with all
flights. IGN BD ORTHO is distributed under the Open Licence:
[official dataset](https://www.data.gouv.fr/datasets/bd-ortho-r).

The viewer shows all acquisition dates returned by the official
`ORTHOIMAGERY.ORTHOPHOTOS.GRAPHE-MOSAIQUAGE:graphe_bdortho` WFS mosaic graph for the
cell extent, separately from the download date. A cell can include multiple
acquisition dates. The France overview is explicitly identified as a multi-date
mosaic. Full attribution, layer names, query provenance and retrieval timestamps
are embedded with the images. The graph’s dates identify source acquisitions,
not flight observation dates.

**Morning / midday / afternoon** opens three aligned panels at the same z level.
Defaults are **08–11, 11–15, 15–18 Europe/Paris**; all four boundaries are editable.
Bands are half-open, so an intersection at 15:00 belongs only to the afternoon.
Changing hour boundaries only filters points in memory. Each panel reports both
intersections and distinct contributing flights; these differ from the all-time
cell population. Empty panels remain visibly empty.

Each cell starts its comparison at its **busiest June–August civil day**, ranked
by distinct crossing flights from both disciplines, independent of segmentation
or climb duration. Ties use the earliest date. **Days before** and **after** pool
consecutive days around that reference; the date is also editable, and **Busiest
summer day** restores the saved recommendation. These comparison dates do not
modify the general From/To interval. Civil days and hour bands use Europe/Paris,
including daylight-saving transitions. All storage timestamps remain UTC.

The figure states the actual inclusive day range and number of pooled days.
Pooled panels show recurring locations at similar local hours across days; they
do not track the identity or displacement of individual thermals. The viewer
reports coverage rather than claiming thermal centres or correcting for uneven
pilot sampling.

To enrich an existing prepared file without rescanning IGC/parquet or rerunning
segmentation:

```bash
uv run --group viewer python scripts/pipeline/prepare_thermal_daily.py
```

The full `prepare_thermal_planes.py` workflow also includes this enrichment.
Point products resume per flight, commit in batches and advertise the completed
lattice only after coverage verification; backgrounds resume per image. The
existing snapshot remains readable if offline enrichment is interrupted. The
writer uses the same `.prepare.lock` as the original preparation. Read-only
viewers never repair or rebuild missing products.

## Enlarged maps and full screen

The daily comparison shows **morning, midday and afternoon in one horizontal
row**, with matching axes. The **View** selector can show the three periods,
France alone, or one chosen period alone. In whole-interval mode it offers
France plus the horizontal plane, France alone, or the horizontal plane alone.
Changing **View** uses the loaded results and preserves dates, z and segmentation.

**Full screen**, at the top right of the application, enlarges the active tab and
hides the flight-picker sidebar. Settings remain above the maps. It works in the
Trajectory, Map and Thermal planes tabs. **Esc**, **F11**, or **Exit full screen**
returns to the previous window layout. Choose a single map in **View** before
entering full screen to dedicate the plotting area to that map.

Controls are compact: only the active time-selection controls are shown. The
long ground-reference summary is available through **Cell details**; acquisition
dates and map credits remain visible. Switching views or entering full screen
requires no cache preparation and does not recompute scientific results.
