\begin{frame}{GNSS supplies altitude; pressure has a separate witness role}
\begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
\column{.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Adopted altitude channel}\par\medskip
\small One channel throughout the population: GNSS presence $\ge95\%$ and range $\ge30$ m at flight level.\par\medskip
Missing or invalid GNSS altitude becomes missing $z$; horizontal coordinates remain subject to their own tests.\par\medskip
The barometer does not substitute for GNSS altitude.
\column{.48\textwidth}
\textbf{Locally available corroboration}\par
\[
 B_I=B_{\rm flight}\prod_{i\in I}b_i.
\]
\small Here $b_i=1$ for a finite, nonzero pressure reading at fix $i$, and $0$ otherwise.\par\medskip
\small The candidate interval needs simultaneous position and pressure readings. An incomplete local witness abstains.
\end{columns}
\question{How sensitive are witness-dependent decisions to the availability of paired pressure observations?}
\source{Eligible barometer: flight presence $\ge95\%$, range $\ge30$ m. Eligibility occurs in \StatPipeParaBaroWitnessPct\% para / \StatPipeHangBaroWitnessPct\% hang flights attempted by the pipeline.}
\speaker{The local pressure requirement answers the concern about GNSS lock. A pressure observation must accompany the horizontal fix when used; it must not be interpolated into the candidate interval. Valid GNSS altitude is not an additional requirement for that witness, since its failure may be the reason pressure is useful. A global rule discarding every unpaired fix would select devices and discard horizontal information unnecessarily. GNSS altitude is a consistent operational channel choice, not a claim that it is always less noisy than pressure altitude. Different height datums across recorders remain unresolved.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A common altitude channel does not resolve its vertical datum}
\small

\[

 \quad\Rightarrow\quad
 \Delta z_{\rm logged}=\Delta z_{\rm reference}
 \quad\text{only when }b\text{ is constant.}
\]

\section{Fix-level decisions}

\begin{frame}{Timestamp and coordinate bookkeeping precede geometric decisions}
\small
Duplicate timestamps are consolidated; backward clock steps, impossible coordinates
and missing positions are treated explicitly. Exact position repetition also enters
the frozen-run witness.
\par\medskip
A plausible-looking coordinate can still have the wrong temporal support. Conversely,
a sharp turn can disagree with a local median without being a recording defect.
\question{Which structural failures can be detected directly, and which require evidence about the physical trajectory?}
\speaker{Use the repository distinction between on-the-trend structural defects and off-the-trend geometric defects. The parser decodes a record; cleaning determines admissible ordering and physical reachability. A local median alone cannot establish whether a flight manoeuvre is real.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A Hampel flag describes a departure; the reach/rejoin rule removes it}
\centering\fig[56mm]{defect-position.pdf}
\question{Which cases can contaminate the median without defeating the reachability check?}
\source{Real flight 20030236; the gate uses geodetic distances. \hyperlink{hampel-backup}{Identifier formula and assumptions}.}
\speaker{A median-based geometric flag and an absolute reachability decision answer different questions. In the actual example the removed point is far off the local course. But a tight turn can also differ from a local median, so the flag alone must not remove it. A corrupt block longer than the local window can move the median into the corruption; requiring both tests would then miss it. Any impossible step left after the scan becomes a boundary. The initial anchor needs a separate whole-flight reach check because the forward scan does not reconsider it.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=hampel-backup]{The identifier's scale is a convention, not a false-alarm probability}
\small
\[
 r_k=d\big((\varphi_k,\lambda_k),
           (\widetilde\varphi_k,\widetilde\lambda_k)\big),\quad
 \sigma_k=1.4826\operatorname{median}_{|t_j-t_k|\le20\,\mathrm s}r_j.
\]
\[
 \text{Flag when } r_k>\max(5\sigma_k,15\ \mathrm m),
 \quad\text{with at least five fixes in the window}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
 \item Component-wise medians define the local reference position; residuals use horizontal great-circle distance.
 \item The factor 1.4826 is the one-dimensional Gaussian MAD normalization. For these radial residuals it is an operational scale convention.
 \item The absolute reach/rejoin gate acts independently of this diagnostic flag.
\end{itemize}
\source{Identifier terminology: \href{https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1993.10476339}{Davies \& Gather (1993)}, Example 2.2. Thresholds and GPS removal rules are choices of this analysis.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{A frozen-position candidate is removed and split}
\centering\fig[56mm]{defect-frozen.pdf}
\question{How would genuine slow motion differ from this frozen-position signature?}
\source{Real flight 20050872; the removed interval remains a gap. \hyperlink{witness-backup}{Complete witness branches}.}
\speaker{The example stays in a small spatial region while time advances. Extent, duration and witness must agree. An exact coordinate repeat supplies a separate signature. With an eligible barometer, its local coverage must be complete and the first/last quarter medians close. Without an eligible barometer, a majority of invalid GNSS declarations or missing GNSS altitudes is the fallback. With globally eligible but locally incomplete pressure, the barometric branch abstains instead of switching fallback. Quantization, genuine slow motion and shared logger failures are possible confounders.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[label=witness-backup]{The complete frozen-position witness}
\small Require $D_I<15$ m, $T_I\ge60$ s and $W_I=1$. Here $D_I$ is the horizontal bounding-box diagonal.\par\medskip Let $E_I$ mean exact coordinate repetition; $d_I$ is the fraction with invalid GNSS declaration or missing GNSS altitude. Then
\[
 W_I=E_I\ \lor\
 \begin{cases}
  |\Delta b_I|<5\ \mathrm m,& B_I=1,\\
  \mathrm{false},& B_{\rm flight}=1,\ B_I=0,\\
  d_I>1/2,& B_{\rm flight}=0.
 \end{cases}
\]
$\Delta b_I$ is the last-quarter minus first-quarter pressure-altitude median.\par\medskip
For interior ground detection, the pressure test instead uses a detrended 5--95\% span and a slope guard, with sustained low speed for at least 10 min. Missing local pressure always causes that guard to abstain.
\source{Small net pressure change can hide an excursion; distinct sensors do not guarantee independent recorder errors. Shorter qualifying slow intervals are flagged and retained.}
\end{frame}

\section{Trimming and flight selection}

\begin{frame}{Outer trimming uses a sustained horizontal-speed criterion}
\[
 v_{xy}>\SI{\PreprocTakeoffSpeedMps}{\metre\per\second}
 \quad\text{for}\quad \SI{\PreprocSustainedS}{\second}.
\]
\small Read forward for onset and backward for the final airborne interval.
Observed step speeds supply the evidence; slow periods inside the flight do not
set these outer boundaries.
\par\medskip
Slow airborne motion can be clipped at an end, while sustained fast ground movement
can be retained. The persistence time is not an error bound on take-off or landing.
\question{Which boundary cases should be inspected independently of the algorithm's own decision?}
\speaker{There is no claim that the onset is accurate to thirty seconds. A logger may record ground movement before takeoff or a long stationary period after landing. Flight records with no sustained high-speed stretch are rejected at this stage.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Time removed before onset and after landing has a long tail}
\centering\fig[60mm]{trim_split_seconds.pdf}
\question{Do the longest removed intervals reflect continued ground logging or slow flight?}
\speaker{These counts cover flights reaching a defined trimming window. They do not describe every catalogue entry or only the final retained population. The raw census was rescanned under the current implementation; the cached and rescanned outputs agreed exactly. A long discarded duration is not automatically a large boundary error.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interior ground intervals require joint speed and barometric evidence}
\small
At least ten minutes of low horizontal speed, complete eligible pressure coverage,
a small detrended pressure-altitude span, and a bounded fitted vertical trend.
\par\medskip
Detrending alone must not erase a genuine steady climb. Without adequate barometry,
the detector abstains.

\question{Can ridge or thermal flight meet these conditions, and how would an independent label distinguish it?}
\speaker{The rule removes an interval and imposes a segment boundary, rather than filling it. There are 231 affected paraglider flights and three hang-glider flights in the current trimming census. Shorter qualifying intervals reaching the frozen-duration threshold are flagged but retained; their planned sensitivity analysis has not yet been performed.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Interior excisions affect a small part of the full trimming census}
\centering\fig[59mm]{trim_interior_excisions.pdf}
\question{Does a small affected count also imply a small effect on phase statistics?}
\speaker{There are 231 affected paraglider and three hang-glider records among those reaching the trimming census. The count is not an independently labelled false-positive rate. Every excision creates a boundary.}
\end{frame}

\section{Coordinates and supported segments}

\begin{frame}{Convert geographic coordinates before measuring horizontal motion}
\[
 (\varphi,\lambda,h)\longrightarrow
 \mathbf r_{\rm ECEF}\longrightarrow
 \begin{pmatrix}E\\N\\U\end{pmatrix}
 =R(\varphi_0,\lambda_0)
   [\mathbf r_{\rm ECEF}-\mathbf r_{{\rm ECEF},0}].
\]
\small The local orthonormal basis is fixed at the flight origin. East and north
are measured in metres, avoiding subtraction of latitude and longitude as if they
were Cartesian distances.
\question{On which route lengths would a fixed tangent frame materially affect a geographical comparison?}
\speaker{Separate the coordinate transformation from the altitude channel used for vertical analysis. The geodesy appendix derives the ECEF/ENU rotation and its geometry. A fixed local frame does not follow the changing tangent plane along a very long route.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The local basis uses the origin's geodetic coordinates}
\small
\[
R(\varphi_0,\lambda_0)=
\begin{pmatrix}
-\sin\lambda_0 & \cos\lambda_0 & 0\\
-\sin\varphi_0\cos\lambda_0 & -\sin\varphi_0\sin\lambda_0 & \cos\varphi_0\\
\cos\varphi_0\cos\lambda_0 & \cos\varphi_0\sin\lambda_0 & \sin\varphi_0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Subtract the origin in ECEF, then rotate. Geodetic latitude follows the ellipsoid
normal; geocentric latitude follows a radius from the Earth's centre.
\par\medskip
The IGC latitude already supplies the geodetic angle used in this transformation.
\question{A coordinate convention can create an apparent displacement error before any physical model is fitted.}
\speaker{This is a fixed orthonormal rotation of the three-dimensional ECEF displacement, not an independently refitted frame at each lag. On the ellipsoid, geodetic and geocentric latitude differ. The complete derivation and approximations belong to the thesis geodesy appendix and can be discussed without treating the Earth as locally flat over arbitrary distances.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Rotated Up and the recorded altitude are different coordinates}
\[
 (E,N,U)=R\,[\mathbf r_{\rm ECEF}-\mathbf r_{{\rm ECEF},0}],
 \qquad z=h_{\rm GNSS,logged}.
\]
\small The stored vertical channel is logged altitude, not height above the origin's
fixed tangent plane. It is not reset to zero at launch.
\par\medskip
GNSS altitude supplies a height proxy for the ECEF conversion. Temporary height
interpolation used by that conversion does not validate a missing vertical interval;
the later gap rule still controls which complete trajectory segments survive.
\question{Which effects depend on an absolute altitude datum, and which cancel in within-segment increments?}
\speaker{Keeping this distinction avoids interpreting Earth-curvature sagitta as a physical descent. A constant vertical bias cancels from vertical differences; spatial or temporal changes do not. The implementation preserves missing-altitude support for subsequent resampling decisions even when a height proxy was needed to calculate horizontal coordinates.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Recorder cadence varies across the raw archive}
\centering\fig[59mm]{sampling_intervals.pdf}
\question{Which native cadences resolve the shortest scales used later?}
\speaker{Preprocessing retains the native median cadence, while Chapter 3 applies a separate common ten-second grid to eligible segments. Horizontal interpolation is shape-preserving cubic and altitude interpolation linear, within the bounded support. A finer numerical grid would not restore missing physical resolution.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The same duration bound governs horizontal and vertical support}
\[
 g_{\max}=\min\!\left(10\Delta t,\ \max(20\,\mathrm s,2\Delta t)\right).
\]
\small
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}\toprule
Native cadence $\Delta t$ & 1 s & 5 s & 15 s\\
Gap bound $g_{\max}$ & 10 s & 20 s & 30 s\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At or below the bound: interpolate and flag. Above it: split the complete trajectory.
For altitude, compare consecutive finite source readings, not the length of a flag run.
\question{How do threshold changes affect both reconstructed motion and which long-lag windows survive?}
\speaker{The cap is not universally twenty seconds. Equality at the actual cadence-dependent bound is admitted. A long altitude-only hole removes the intermediate horizontal fixes as well; missing prefixes and suffixes are excluded without extrapolation. Mandatory cleaning boundaries always remain boundaries.}
\end{frame}

The observed path length sums within segments. The net displacement between the

\section{Smoothing and differentiation}

\begin{frame}{One local polynomial estimates position and derivatives}
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.59\textwidth}\centering\fig[48mm]{sg-fit.pdf}
\column{.38\textwidth}\small
For offsets $u=-m,\ldots,m$,
\[
 P_i(u)=\sum_{k=0}^3 a_k(i)u^k,
\]
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat x_i&=a_0,\\
 \widehat v_i&=a_1/\Delta t,\\
 \widehat a_i&=2a_2/\Delta t^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The same fit is applied separately to $E,N,z$ inside each segment.
\end{columns}
\question{Smoothing controls derivative noise, but it also changes resolved motion and propagates reconstructed values through its windows.}
\source{Synthetic illustration of the implemented Savitzky--Golay fit. \href{https://doi.org/10.1021/ac60214a047}{Savitzky \& Golay (1964)}.}
\speaker{The polynomial is fitted in sample offsets, so physical derivatives require division by cadence and its square. A cubic is the present modelling choice; a quadratic would already permit nonzero acceleration. The coefficients are computed using the numerical library rather than explicitly inverting a normal-equation matrix. The example is synthetic and only illustrates the estimator. Real GNSS noise can be correlated and nonstationary, and a plausible filtered curve is not proof that a manoeuvre has been preserved.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The nominal 5-s timescale does not mean a 5-s window for every logger}
\begin{columns}[c,onlytextwidth]
\column{.58\textwidth}\centering\fig[48mm]{sg-response.pdf}
\column{.39\textwidth}\small
\[
 w=\max\{\lceil5\,\mathrm s/\Delta t\rceil_{\rm odd},5\}.
\]
Five samples span:\par\medskip
\begin{tabular}{@{}rr@{}}\toprule
Cadence & Window span\\\midrule
1 s & 4 s\\
3 s & 12 s\\
5 s & 20 s\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par\medskip
The filter's frequency response depends on cadence.
\end{columns}
\question{Quantify the sensitivity of 10-s transport and phase features to cadence and the smoothing window.}
\source{The observed spectral knee motivates a working timescale; it is not the exact cutoff of the Savitzky--Golay filter. The response shown is the interior value-filter response.}
\speaker{Even though the configured timescale is five seconds, the minimum five-sample window can span twenty seconds for a five-second logger. This makes the 10-second endpoint of Chapter 3 potentially sensitive to preprocessing. The current horizontal and vertical timescales are separate parameters that happen to have equal values. The exact filter response was computed from the adopted coefficients. What is still missing is a targeted comparison of the scientific observables under alternative cadences and windows, not another statement that the filter is smooth.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The value filter changes the contribution of position noise}
\centering\fig[52mm]{savgol_response.pdf}
\small Left: the interior value-filter response. Right: its MSD contribution for
independent white position noise of variance $\sigma^2$, in one coordinate.
\question{How would correlated recording errors change this illustrative noise calculation?}
\speaker{The right panel is a stated white-noise model, not a measured decomposition of the flight MSD. Neither panel plots the derivative transfer function. Position and derivatives come from the same local polynomial but have different coefficients. A nominal five-second timescale gives a five-sample minimum window, whose physical span depends on cadence.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Smoothing modifies the measured displacement variance}
\[
\widehat x_j=\sum_k c_k x_{j+k},\qquad
\Delta_\ell\widehat x_j=\sum_k c_k\Delta_\ell x_{j+k}.
\]
\small Consequently,
\[
 \mathbb E[(\Delta_\ell\widehat x)^2]
 =\sum_{k,m}c_kc_m\,
   \mathbb E[\Delta_\ell x_{j+k}\Delta_\ell x_{j+m}].
\]
The effect depends on correlations as well as the filter coefficients. A visually
smoother trajectory does not establish that its short-lag MSD is unchanged.
\question{On a common set of flights, which fitted ranges remain stable under cadence and window changes?}
\speaker{This identity is for an interior linear convolution; boundary fitting needs separate treatment. It does not assume independent increments or declare the unfiltered observation to be the true path. The sensitivity comparison must separate changes to measured motion from changes to the set of admitted flights.}
\end{frame}
