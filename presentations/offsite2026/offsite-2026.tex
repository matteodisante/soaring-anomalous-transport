\notetext{A flight is sustained by an energy budget: the pilot trades altitude for horizontal distance, searches for rising air, and recovers altitude in lift. These are useful behavioural descriptions, not phase labels supplied by the recorder or results of a segmentation shown here. Ground motion includes both air-relative motion and wind. The challenge is to explain whole-flight transport while respecting these physical constraints. The present measurements are the first layer of that programme. An MSD exponent alone cannot distinguish all the candidate mechanisms: persistent motion, changes of behaviour and heterogeneous conditions can produce similar second moments. The schematic is illustrative and its horizontal axis has no numerical scale. Source: thesis introduction, Sections 1.1--1.3.}

\notetext{GNSS height is the chosen output channel, used throughout. Its absolute reference needs no pressure or temperature calibration, whereas pressure altitude shifts with the weather. In supported paired records, high-frequency median spectra are comparable to barometry. This does not make GNSS uniformly more accurate: logged vertical datums (ellipsoid or geoid) remain unaudited. Barometry corroborates cleaning decisions without replacing the output altitude.\par\medskip A fix records time, position and altitude at one instant. The rule is conservative: a fix is deleted only when the flight without it is physically consistent. In the left example, A is at 0 m at 0 s, B at 800 m at 1 s, and C at 10 m at 2 s. Reaching B from A requires an impossible 800 m/s. Removing B leaves 10 m in 2 s: 5 m/s. Deletion therefore passes both the impossibility and rejoin tests. The dashed chord tests reachability; it is not interpolation. Contiguous blocks are handled the same way. In the right example the track jumps and stays displaced: no later fix is reachable from the last accepted one within 20 seconds, the Hampel half-window. Unlike the single spike on the left, the displacement persists past the window, so it cannot be an isolated error. The pipeline then keeps both branches, segments 1 and 2, and splits the flight at the jump without deleting any fix, because it cannot tell which branch holds the true positions. A Hampel flag alone never deletes a fix; timestamp, frozen-lock and altitude defects have separate rules.\par\medskip The timeline distinguishes take-off, intermediate landing, relaunch and final landing. Grey ground phases are removed; the blue pieces, segments 1 and 2, remain segments of one parent flight. Outer boundaries use sustained speed. No log marks a landing: stops are inferred from the time series of position and altitude. Interior stops also require eligible barometry supporting stable altitude: slow motion alone is insufficient. An excised stop becomes a boundary that later processing never bridges. Sources: thesis Sections 2.7--2.8 and cleaning ADRs.}

Shared wind and rise speed translate a source pattern; variability across dates can broaden pooled crossings. Matched climbs and wind profiles are needed to test this.\par\medskip Terrain guidance and clearance could interrupt open routes' outward progress. Longer legs or later returns could weaken closure's signature in closed routes. Both may narrow the circuit gap; route timing remains untested. Sources: FAA Glider Flying Handbook, Chapters 9--10; SSA, \href{https://www.ssa.org/lift-sources/}{Lift Sources}; Schroeder and Buck, Fire Weather, Chapter 6; thesis Section 3.2.2.}

Averaging and dividing by mean squared speed gives the displayed normalised velocity autocorrelation under stationary sampling. Aligned directions contribute positively, perpendicular directions give zero and reversals contribute negatively.
