This monotone admission rule differs from Figure 3.1: that figure uses the general population and requires exact native short-lag resolution, so its short-lag counts need not grow monotonically. From ten seconds, this slide uses the thesis's common ten-second grid and reproduces every original MSD point.\par\medskip There are \CadenceLagCount\ evaluated lags, including every second from one to ten. Local H is half the log--log slope in a plus/minus 0.25-decade window, expanded to the nearest three lags when sparse. The one-second estimate uses one to three seconds. Slopes below ten seconds, and windows crossing that boundary, combine dynamics with changing sample composition. Lines connect computed estimates.\par\medskip The original 10--10,000 second fits and 1,000 paired site--day bootstrap draws are preserved: H=\CadenceParaH\ [\CadenceParaLow,\CadenceParaHigh] and \CadenceHangH\ [\CadenceHangLow,\CadenceHangHigh]. Bands are pointwise 90 percent intervals. Sources: thesis Section 3.1; cadence-support-report.json and measure\_cadence\_support.py.}

\notetext{The reported confidence intervals use the current site--day cluster bootstrap. Each group combines flights from the same declared take-off site and calendar day. We sample groups with replacement, preserve all flights belonging to a sampled group, recompute the equal-flight estimator, and repeat this one thousand times. The same multiplicities are paired across lags and conditional comparisons. The current partition is too fine: nearby sites and neighbouring days can share weather and other conditions, so flights in different groups can still be dependent. In the current cohort, 71.1 percent of paraglider groups and 81.3 percent of hang-glider groups are singletons. These are diagnostic comparisons, not a validated replacement partition, and other chapter bands have not been recomputed with that scheme.
