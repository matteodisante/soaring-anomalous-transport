# Chapter 3 — Global transport: prioritised observable inventory

## What the chapter establishes, in order

Chapter 3 establishes, in this order: (0) a statistical contract — effective sample size, clustered errors, a declared resolution budget, and a pre-registered split-half — under which every later number is judged; (1) that the about-take-off ensemble MSD is **not** a transport observable but a measurement of the population, whose landmarks (slope minimum ~137 s, super-ballistic peak ~1500 s, everything beyond ~3000 s) are departure-time and duration-composition effects, and which is therefore retired as a source of exponents; (2) that the object which *can* carry an exponent is the within-segment, stationary-increment, trend-robust family (filtered variations and wavelet variance, of which the TAMSD is the p=1 special case), and what that exponent is with an honest interval; (3) how many regimes that curve supports and where they change — as a calibrated breakpoint count with a null for spurious breaks, a crossover *sharpness*, and the dual answer in length rather than time; (4) whether that structure is a property of the motion or manufactured by the population, the environment or the scoring rule, via replay, quenched-environment, closed-circuit and pair-dispersion nulls; (5) what the propagator does beyond its second moment — semigroup consistency, front, tail threshold, ergodicity-breaking shape; (6) where the memory and the space–time coupling live; (7) the mechanism, by conditioning horizontal transport on the coarse-grained vertical state without ever assigning a label; and (8) a joint generative model plus a short consistency table with pre-declared tolerances, which is the falsifiable form of "how many regimes".

**Resolution budget, declared up front and repeated in every caption.** Ensemble curve 121–13021 s (2.03 dec), time-averaged 121–6341 s (1.72 dec); residuals are correlated across lags because every lag averages the same flights, so effective d.o.f. per curve is 4–6, not 80. Therefore: K=1 breakpoint is estimable, K=2 is marginal, **K≥3 is not claimable**. Any exponent quoted tighter than ±0.02 is a reporting error.

## §0 — The statistical contract (protocols, not methods; applied everywhere)

**P1. Pre-registered split-half.** ESSENTIAL — fix the regime count, breakpoint positions and fit windows on half the flights (split by day×site, not by flight), then confirm out-of-sample on the other half; report both. *Adds:* the only defence against 25 methods × 5 d.o.f.; costs nothing at n=155,788. *Pitfall:* the split must be frozen before any curve is looked at, and the fold assignment committed to git.

**P2. Clustered bootstrap + lag-covariance.** ESSENTIAL — resample at day×site (join `catalog.csv` date/takeoff/pilot on `flight_id`), report the intra-class correlation of per-flight α at flight / day / day×site / pilot / season so the level is justified by measurement; all fits are GLS under the bootstrap covariance of log MSD across lags. *Adds:* replaces the ±0.001 on α_TA, which is wrong by 1–2 orders of magnitude, and the `local_slope` IQR/√n band, which measures window straightness rather than uncertainty. *Pitfall:* anonymised catalog rows become singleton clusters and bias N_eff *upward* — report the anonymised fraction and give the all-singleton and all-pooled bounds.

**P3. Cadence–filter protocol.** ESSENTIAL — one protocol, not three: declare the smoothing scale w as an analysis parameter, coarse-grain every observable to a common w, and validate by **decimating the 1 Hz flights to 2/5/10 s with re-smoothing at the decimated cadence**. *Adds:* removes the confound in the existing cadence control (three cohorts of different flights, α spread 0.06 para / 0.78 hang) and makes the 30 % slow-logger population usable. *Pitfall:* the Savitzky–Golay window is fixed at 5 *samples*, so v is a 5 s derivative at 1 Hz and a 50 s one at 10 s (sd(v_z) 2.08→1.54, sd(|v_h|) 4.12→5.48) — nothing velocity-derived may be pooled across cadence classes.

**P4. Cohort and weighting convention.** ESSENTIAL — every statistic reported on duration cohorts (≥1 h / ≥2 h / ≥4 h, already accumulated) and under both flight-equal and increment-pooled weighting; flight-equal is the default given the measured 40 % / 62 % stratum incompatibility. *Adds:* fixes once, for the whole chapter, the composition confound that otherwise re-litigates in every section. *Pitfall:* cohorts hold duration fixed but discard recombination — quote them as a stability check, not as the corrected curve.

**P5. Hang-glider scope.** ESSENTIAL — 6,132 flights (2,069 baro & dt≤2 s; 1,152 with T≥3 h) support a **replication check on three headline numbers**, not a parallel characterisation. *Pitfall:* the 31.2 % ensemble residual and 0.78 cadence α-spread already say that curve is not measuring one thing; do not stratify it further.

## §1 — The ensemble MSD is a measurement of the population

The chapter opens by demoting its own most striking figure. A super-ballistic local slope of 2.21 is impossible for any stationary-increment process; the word "exponent" is removed from that caption.

**1.1 Anchor-varied ensemble MSD.** ESSENTIAL — recompute ⟨|r(t)−r(t_dep)|²⟩ with the clock zeroed at each flight's own departure event (first 5 km crossing, median 1911 s, IQR 1334–2739, available for 95.1 % para / 96.4 % hang), then landing-anchored with the clock reversed, then anchored at a common solar time; compare the number and position of slope breaks across anchors. *Adds:* turns the established explanation of the 1500 s bump into a constructive test and produces the curve that should actually be quoted. *Pitfall:* departure anchoring costs the median departure time off the top, shrinking the range 2.03→~1.8 dec; the ~5 % that never cross 5 km are a separate stratum, not a deletion.

**1.2 Composition and influence.** ESSENTIAL — per lag: n_flights, participation ratio (inverse Herfindahl) of the per-flight |r(t)|² contributions, top-1 % weight share, and the shift in break positions when the top contributors / one site / one season are dropped; plus the cohort tracking already latent in `thesis/generated/msd_curve.csv`. *Adds:* the decisive number already on disk — the slope minimum sits at 137 s in all three cohorts and the peak at 1504/1504/1695 s, but the secondary dip moves 3481→5624→3481 s and the second peak 8059→9086→4425 s; that single comparison sets the honest ceiling of the chapter. *Pitfall:* N_eff counts increments, participation counts weight — report both or the argument is contestable.

**1.3 Flight-duration survival and hazard.** ESSENTIAL — S(T) and h(T) per discipline, season and orographic group, from `flights_meta.duration_flight_s` (no pass needed), overlaid on every ensemble curve. *Adds:* the explicit censoring model for the whole chapter, and the falsifiable check that the 1500 s feature does *not* coincide with a hazard feature; the hazard also carries the end of the convective day, which the honesty note currently only asserts. *Pitfall:* the 2400 s pipeline floor and the 57,600 s cap are artefacts of the pipeline, not of the sport — mark them on the axis.

**1.4 First passage from take-off, Kaplan–Meier.** VALUABLE — T_q(r) to a log radius grid with right-censoring at flight end; this is the exact dual of 1.1–1.3, not of §3. *Adds:* converts the population landmark into a *length* (the 5 km/1911 s point becomes one point of a curve) and handles thinning by an explicit survival estimator instead of a silently biased partial average. *Pitfall:* it is the same random variable as the mean maximal excursion — only the MME/MSD **ratio** may appear, and neither may occupy a consistency-table row as an independent exponent.

*Presentation convention (not a method): every curve in the chapter is shown compensated — MSD/t^α_ref and, within a cadence class only, MSD/(v_rms²t²) — so that a regime is a plateau and a crossover is a stationary point.*

## §2 — The estimator that can carry an exponent

Within-segment, stationary-increment, trend-robust. Everything downstream is built here, not on §1.

**2.1 Filtered (higher-order) variations, order scan p = 1,2,3.** ESSENTIAL — V_p(Δ)=E|A_p r(Δ)|² with p vanishing moments, rotation-invariant by summing E and N; Ĥ_p from the two-scale ratio ½log₂[V_p(2Δ)/V_p(Δ)] as well as from a fit; plus Coeurjolly's quantile/MAD variant. *Adds:* converts the plan's binary raw/centred drift treatment into a convergent sequence — Ĥ₁−Ĥ₂ measures how much of the exponent is course, Ĥ₂=Ĥ₃ certifies nothing polynomial is left; the lag where p=1 and p=2 separate is itself the scale above which the motion is locally straight. It is the existing `MSDAccumulator` with a different kernel. *Pitfall:* must be built on within-segment increments (TAMSD geometry), never on |r(t)|² about take-off; p=2 costs a factor 2 at the long end (~20–3000 s).

**2.2 Wavelet variance / logscale diagram (Abry–Veitch), vanishing-moment scan.** ESSENTIAL — Daubechies-3/4 within each segment, log₂⟨d²_{j,·}⟩ vs octave j, weighted regression, and the **χ² goodness-of-fit for "these octaves lie on one line"** scanned over (j₁,j₂). *Adds:* the only estimator here with a real statistical theory — per-octave analytic intervals, a properly weighted fit, and a principled number-of-scaling-ranges procedure instead of reading a local slope by eye; best long-scale reach of any trend-robust estimator (~2 s to ~8000 s pooled). *Pitfall:* the χ² intervals assume quasi-decorrelated coefficients *and independent flights* — the octave-level errors must still be widened by P2.

**2.3 Re-audit of the time-averaged power law.** ESSENTIAL — replace the rms-residual test with a residual **sign-structure** test (Wald–Wolfowitz runs, and the sup of the local-slope excursion against the simultaneous band). *Adds:* corrects a headline claim at zero cost — on 121–6341 s the TA local slope runs 1.89 → 1.644 → 2.05 and the 34 residuals form 4 sign runs where ~18 are expected (z≈−4.8); the TA curve is a systematic arch, and its minimum moves 1695→2739→5624 s across duration cohorts, which marks it as censoring-driven. *Pitfall:* residual magnitude is the wrong statistic; do not let 3.8 % rms survive anywhere in the text as evidence of a single regime.

**2.4 Lag-resolved drift/fluctuation decomposition, out-of-sample.** ESSENTIAL — at each lag, the share carried by ⟨v_d²⟩t² against the fluctuation about it, with the drift estimated on the first half of each flight and applied to the second. *Adds:* locates on the lag axis where ballistic drift takes over (itself a candidate boundary) and quantifies the in-sample detrending bias that manufactures spurious long-lag flattening. *Pitfall:* in-sample net-drift removal turns the walk into a bridge — this is the reason §5 and the persistence tests must use the split-sample estimator.

## §3 — How many regimes, and where

Run on §2's curves. The ensemble curve enters here only as the population comparison from §1.

**3.1 Local slope with a flight/cluster bootstrap band, calibrated by a window-width ladder.** ESSENTIAL — resample flights (clustered per P2) from `MSDAccumulator.stacked_samples` / `TAMSDAccumulator.stacked_samples`, recompute the whole curve and its local slope per replicate, take pointwise percentiles **plus a sup-norm-calibrated simultaneous band**; recompute at half-widths 0.05/0.10/0.15/0.25/0.40 dex and track every extremum across widths (Lindeberg-style). *Adds:* turns the panel that shows the answer into a test, and converts the 0.15 dex "compromise" into a measurement of which extrema are width-robust and how wide each bend is in decades. *Pitfall:* the existing band (IQR of within-window pairwise Theil–Sen slopes / √n) measures straightness, not uncertainty, and must be deleted, not supplemented; above ~0.4 dex too few independent windows remain.

**3.2 Calibrated breakpoint inference.** ESSENTIAL — continuous piecewise-linear in log–log with K **selected** (BIC/CV under the bootstrap covariance), exact dynamic programming over the lag grid (not Muggeo iteration, which has multiple optima here), percentile CIs on breakpoints from the clustered bootstrap, **and a null distribution of spurious K** from surrogates sharing the real N_eff(lag), duration censoring and lag-to-lag correlation but containing no break. *Adds:* the literal sentence the chapter needs — "two regimes, crossing at τ [CI] s" — with a calibrated false-positive rate; agreement of the fitted breakpoint with the wavelet χ² range boundary is corroboration. *Pitfall:* K≥3 is not separable from one broad bend at 1.7–2.0 dec; declare the ceiling before fitting, and never quote a breakpoint better than ±6 % in t (0.05 dex grid).

**3.3 Smooth two-branch crossover with free sharpness.** ESSENTIAL — MSD = A t^a₁[1+(t/τ)^w]^((a₂−a₁)/w), with w and its **profile-likelihood** CI, and the crossover width in decades compared against the width of the branches it separates. *Adds:* the kink-vs-bend discriminant no other method supplies, plus the decision rule — if the crossover is as wide as either branch there are no two regimes, only one curved function and an effective exponent on a declared range. *Pitfall:* the parameters are badly non-quadratic; covariance-matrix CIs are meaningless here, and the three-branch extension is not defensible on 1.7 dec.

**3.4 Moving-origin exit time from balls, T_q(r) and z(r)=dlogT_q/dlogr.** ESSENTIAL — exit from a disc of radius r centred at r(t₀), over strided t₀ within segments; median and quartiles, right-censored. *Adds:* the only answer expressed as a **length** (turn radius, thermal spacing ~2–5 km, valley ~10–20 km, glide range) rather than a time, and comparing 1/Ĥ_FPT against §2's H is a direct test of self-similarity itself. *Pitfall:* the about-take-off version is the contaminated §1 observable and must not be substituted; stride ≥ r/v_typ so consecutive starts are not the same excursion; **negative-order and small-r moments are excluded** — below the smoothing scale they measure the filter kernel, not trapping.

**3.5 Scale-free quantile-ratio shape index R_p(Δ)=q_p/q_0.5, with r*(Δ) derived.** ESSENTIAL — p = 0.75/0.9/0.95/0.99 on the drift-centred increments; flat in lag ⇔ self-similar propagator, whatever the exponent; the lags where R_p moves are boundaries, and the p at which core scaling gives way to front scaling converts to a radius r*(Δ). *Adds:* a fit-free, cutoff-free, binning-free regime locator that reads *shape* rather than variance — it can find a crossover where the second moment is smooth and refuse one where it bends; supersedes quantile-slope and bi-scaling entries, which are the same numbers read twice. *Pitfall:* p=0.999 needs ~10⁴ increments per lag — out of reach above ~3000 s (para) and entirely for hang gliders (cap at 0.99).

## §4 — Is the structure in the motion, or in the population, the environment, or the scoring rule?

**4.1 Population-matched replay, with an injected-α positive control.** ESSENTIAL — 2-D persistent random walks with per-flight speed and persistence drawn from the measured distributions, imposed with the archive's duration distribution, cadences, segment gaps, take-off anchoring, departure-time spread and fit windows, pushed through the *production* `MSDAccumulator`/`TAMSDAccumulator`; the same chain with a known injected α gives bias and power at the real N_eff. *Adds:* the only test that can falsify the anomalous-transport claim outright rather than flag a discrepancy, and the only statement of what regime count this chain could detect in principle. **Merges** the "synthetic no-regime surrogate" and the "mixture forward model" — one simulator, parameters drawn or measured. *Pitfall:* 20–30k matched flights suffice; simulating all 155,788 buys nothing and hides the point.

**4.2 Relative dispersion between simultaneous flights (Richardson).** ESSENTIAL *(examiner's addition, accepted as a first-class observable)* — ⟨|r_i(t)−r_j(t)|²⟩ for pairs launched at the same site within a short window, resolved by initial separation. *Adds:* common advection cancels **identically** in the difference, so this is the only estimator immune to both the per-flight drift and the day-level wind; comparing its exponent to the single-particle one measures directly how much superdiffusion is shared air-mass motion. *Pitfall:* positions are in per-flight local ENU frames — pairs must be reprojected from lat0/lon0 to a common frame before any separation is computed; block by day×site to keep the pair count small.

**4.3 Goal-directed / closed-circuit null, and return statistics.** ESSENTIAL *(examiner's addition)* — a noisy traversal of a declared closed task (out-and-return, triangle) produces exactly the observed rise-then-fall in ⟨|r|²⟩ with zero anomalous transport; test it with the return probability P(|r(t)|<ε) and the fraction within R of launch vs t (tail-free, no moments at all), stratified by `catalog.flight_type`, plus a Procrustes comparison against declared task geometry. *Adds:* the leading benign explanation of §1's signature, and the mechanical confound behind the 0.80 anisotropy and any long-lag heading correlation. *Pitfall:* `flight_type` and scoring rules changed across 27 seasons — within-season only.

**4.4 Quenched-environment tests.** VALUABLE *(examiner's addition; I demote from essential on cost)* — (i) bin France on a 2–5 km grid, estimate the ensemble mean flux field ⟨v(x)⟩, and recompute the MSD after subtracting the *field* rather than the per-flight drift; (ii) trajectory reproducibility across **different days at the same site**. *Adds:* every other null in the chapter is annealed; the measured 62 % orographic incompatibility is exactly what frozen-in-space disorder (hotspots, ridges, convergence lines) predicts, and (ii) separates quenched terrain from same-day weather, which the gaggle test cannot. *Pitfall:* the flux field is itself estimated from the same trajectories — use split-half (fit the field on fold A, detrend fold B) or it is circular.

**4.5 Selection tilt and gaggle dependence.** VALUABLE — exponent, EA/TA gap and break positions across **within-season** deciles of `distance_km`/`points`/`speed` and across `flight_type`, with post-stratification reweighting; plus the measured correlation between simultaneous trajectories folded into P2. *Adds:* survivorship bias acting directly on the measured quantity — a monotone rise of α with ladder score means the exponent is a property of the submission cut. *Pitfall:* declared distance carries route multipliers; use it as a stratifier only, never as a path length.

**4.6 Duration-censoring correction.** VALUABLE — IPCW or within-stratum modelling integrated over the unconditional duration distribution, plus the correlation of per-flight (α_i, D_i, T_i). *Adds:* the cohorts show the effect is modest but never produce the corrected curve or an error bar on the correction; and a longer flight is a *better* flight, so this links mechanically to 4.5. *Pitfall:* weights are well behaved to ~4 h and dominated by a handful of flights beyond 8 h — that, not N_eff, is the honest end of the fit range.

## §5 — The propagator beyond its second moment

**5.1 Chapman–Kolmogorov / semigroup consistency.** ESSENTIAL *(examiner's addition, accepted as the strongest omission)* — test whether P(·,Δ₁+Δ₂) equals the convolution of P(·,Δ₁) and P(·,Δ₂), on drift-centred within-segment increments, over the lag pairs where both are well sampled. *Adds:* a model-free, distribution-level memory test at the level of the whole propagator rather than one moment — renewal-with-heavy-tails passes, correlated increments fail; it is the distributional counterpart of everything in §6. *Pitfall:* the convolution must be done in the same drift-centring convention at both lags, and the comparison metric (1-Wasserstein on log|r|) needs a bootstrap null because the two sides are estimated from overlapping data.

**5.2 Ballistic-front / support-edge test with a fitted prefactor.** ESSENTIAL — q_0.99, q_0.999 and max|Δr_c|/Δ vs Δ against the per-flight speed ceiling; and S(r,Δ)=Δ^(−λ)F(r/Δ) with λ **fitted**, not assumed. *Adds:* the Lévy-walk/Lévy-flight discriminator in the only form that can work here — ground speed is bounded, so an uncoupled flight is excluded a priori and the real question is whether the *flight-time* distribution is heavy-tailed; a confirmed ballistic front fixes the front branch at slope 1 and makes the moment fit one-parameter. *Pitfall:* v_max is a Savitzky–Golay quantity — valid within a cadence class only (P3).

**5.3 Mean-excess (POT/GPD) plot at fixed lag.** ESSENTIAL — the mean residual life of |Δr_c(Δ)|, with the threshold r₀(Δ) read off the straight segment; Clauset–Shalizi–Newman/Hill kept only as a confirmatory number. *Adds:* makes the threshold choice visible and turns it into a measured *spatial* boundary r₀(Δ), instead of hiding it inside a criterion a referee can dispute. *Pitfall:* pooled overlapping increments are within-flight correlated — bootstrap over flights, and cross-check against the per-flight-maximum GEV, which is one number per flight and immune to that objection.

**5.4 φ(ξ), the distribution of single-flight time averages, pooled and self-normalised.** ESSENTIAL — the full histogram of ξ=δ²/⟨δ²⟩ at fixed Δ/T, computed both pooled and after normalising each flight by its own amplitude; EB is reported as its second moment, not as a separate method. *Adds:* a shape fingerprint requiring no exponent (concentration at ξ=1 = ergodic; one-sided-Lévy with weight at 0 = CTRW trapping; broad-but-regular = Lévy walk), and the pooled-vs-self-normalised **gap is the measurement** of how much apparent non-ergodicity is amplitude heterogeneity. *Adds nothing further:* the Δ/T collapse is a read-out of the same object. *Pitfall:* duration spans only ~1.25 dec (2400–43,200 s), so at most 4–5 Δ/T bins; hang gliders cannot resolve φ.

**5.5 Non-Gaussianity: within- vs between-flight decomposition, with the NGP peak.** VALUABLE — NGP(Δ)=⟨|Δr_c|⁴⟩/(2⟨|Δr_c|²⟩²)−1 (2-D convention) with a clustered band, its peak lag, a top-0.1 %-truncated variant, and the same statistics recomputed after standardising each flight by its own robust scale s_f(Δ). *Adds:* separates "the propagator is anomalous" from "the archive is a mixture of near-Gaussian flights of different widths" — the leading alternative hypothesis given the measured 40 %/62 % stratum incompatibility; the NGP peak lag is an independent boundary estimate to compare against §3.2. *Pitfall:* s_f(Δ) needs ≥20 non-overlapping increments per flight, so the decomposition is a 120–600 s measurement; beyond that use overlapping increments for the scale only and say so.

**5.6 Lag-resolved exponent surface ζ(q,Δ), q ≤ 3, within a stratum.** OPTIONAL *(partial overrule of the examiner, who kills all q-space work)* — the strong-anomalous bilinear form is a claim about **front vs bulk**, not about multifractality, and the front is physically bounded and therefore testable; but it runs within a wing-class × orographic stratum, never pooled, and it is demoted from "the main test" to a corroboration of 5.2. *Pitfall:* a mixture of monofractals *is* multifractal — any pooled ζ(q) curvature is population mixing and cannot be distinguished from intermittency after the fact.

**5.7 Kramers–Moyal coefficients in scale, D₁(r,Δ), D₂(r,Δ), with a Pawula check on D₄.** OPTIONAL *(overrule: the examiner ranks this with 5.1; I do not)* — regime boundaries as changes in the functional form of the coefficients. *Pitfall:* D₄ estimation and the Markov-in-scale premise both need far more clean range than 1.7 decades; run only if 5.1 passes.

## §6 — Memory, coupling, and ageing

**6.1 Joint turning-angle / step-length distribution P(φ|L,Δ).** ESSENTIAL — on non-overlapping block increments, ⟨cos φ | L⟩ as a function of L at each Δ, with Δ swept 30–1800 s. *Adds:* the space–time coupling that distinguishes a Lévy walk from a Lévy flight, measured at block level with no segmentation; and the Δ at which the turning-angle law stops being bimodal (circling) and becomes forward-peaked is a direct read of the regime-separation timescale with no exponent. *Pitfall:* it is also the condition under which the diagonal/cross decomposition of the MSD is legitimate — state that dependence explicitly rather than testing it via the double shuffle.

**6.2 Two-time VACF C_v(t_a, t_a+τ) and the running Green–Kubo integral D(t).** ESSENTIAL — from the stored v_E, v_N by FFT per segment, restricted to an age window; 1 Hz class only. *Adds:* the cheapest direct test of the stationarity premise that every correlation observable assumes and none tests; and D(t) locates crossovers on a curve whose noise integrates *down*, instead of on a local slope that amplifies scatter ~4×. Its decorrelation time is an **independent prediction** of the §3.2 breakpoint location — if it lands near the first breakpoint, that breakpoint is the persistence crossover, not a transport boundary. *Pitfall:* SG velocities — 1 Hz only, no cross-cadence pooling.

**6.3 Ageing: TAMSD by start age t_a and the ratio Λ(t_a).** ESSENTIAL — age bands 0–600 / 600–1800 / 1800–3600 / 3600–7200 s, each with its own local slope, and the ratio to the youngest band. *Adds:* compares one estimator with itself at two ages, so it survives the ensemble curve not being a power law; and it is the falsifiable test of the departure-spread explanation — post-departure ages must be t_a-independent, and if they are not, there is a second ageing component. *Pitfall:* median duration 9,490 s gives 3–4 usable bands, the oldest thin; report n(t_a,Δ) on the figure.

**6.4 Lagged mutual information I(Δr(t); Δr(t+τ)) against the linear decorrelation time.** VALUABLE *(examiner's addition)* — plus transfer entropy v_z → horizontal straightness. *Adds:* a nonlinear, tail-insensitive decorrelation time whose **difference** from the VACF time is itself a measurement of nonlinear memory; and it upgrades §7.4's lead–lag from correlation to directed information. *Pitfall:* MI estimator bias scales with bin count and sample size — use a k-NN (Kraskov) estimator and validate on the §4.1 replay, where the true answer is known.

**6.5 Occupation and range observables.** VALUABLE *(examiner's addition)* — number of distinct coarse cells visited S(t), convex-hull area and perimeter vs t, mean returns to an ε-ball around r(t₀), and the winding-angle distribution. *Adds:* the only observables sensitive to **re-visiting**, which is the thermalling signature and which no moment of displacement can see; winding angle is a 2-D-only quantity with an exact Brownian reference and is the most direct segmentation-free measure of circling; this is also the currency of the animal-movement literature that will referee the work. *Pitfall:* cell size and ε must be set well above the GPS noise floor and swept; Spitzer's law is a Brownian *reference shape*, not a valid null for this process — do not report a p-value against it.

**6.6 State-dimension test.** VALUABLE *(examiner's addition)* — does conditioning Δr(Δ) on v(t−τ) as well as v(t) improve prediction out-of-sample? *Adds:* "how many dynamical variables" is a different question from "how many regimes", and it is the one that decides whether an HMM is the right object in Chapter 4 — this is the chapter's hand-off. *Pitfall:* SG velocities are already smoothed over 5 samples, so a spurious improvement from filter-induced correlation must be calibrated against decimated surrogates (P3).

**6.7 Sparre Andersen persistence and the arcsine laws.** OPTIONAL *(partial overrule: the examiner kills it; I retain it demoted, with the fix stated)* — the exact parameter-free null is destroyed by in-sample net-drift centring, which converts the walk into a bridge. Retain only with the split-sample drift estimator of 2.4 **and** an explicit demonstration that the residual course is small against the step scale. *Pitfall:* with median wind 3.1–3.5 m/s against 14–15 m/s ground speed (20–25 % residual course), that demonstration will probably fail — if it does, report the failure and drop the test, do not report the exponent.

## §7 — Mechanism: the vertical channel as the switch

**7.1 Horizontal transport conditioned on coarse-grained v̄_z.** ESSENTIAL — net displacement rate, path-length rate and straightness as functions of v̄_z at declared w (swept 30–240 s), plus the conditional TAMSD with lags bucketed by their starting window's v̄_z. *Adds:* the single strongest item in the chapter and the direct answer to where the horizontal crossovers come from, obtained **without a single state label** by conditioning on a continuous covariate; measured at w=60 s: 5.86 m/s net rate in climbing windows against 13.41 m/s in sinking ones, straightness 0.39 vs 0.81. The vertical state is a switch that turns ballistic horizontal motion on and off. *Pitfall:* clean vertical subset only (alt_source=baro **and** dt≤2 s: 93,589 para / 2,069 hang) — sd(v_z) is 2.08 m/s on baro and 4.02 on GNSS, so a 1 Hz GNSS flight's vertical channel is majority noise.

**7.2 Cycle timescale and dwell-time distribution: ⟨Δz²(τ)⟩ saturation + v_z ACF + excursion-length distribution.** ESSENTIAL — three estimators of one number, kept for three different reasons: ⟨Δz²⟩ because it is positions-only and works at every cadence (reuses `time_averaged_msd` on z); the ACF because it adds the **aperiodicity amplitude** (measured: first zero crossing 246 s, secondary maximum at 203 s of amplitude 0.028 — under 3 % of C(0), i.e. strongly aperiodic, so dwell times are broadly distributed); the threshold-swept level-crossing excursions because they give the **distribution** the Lévy-walk reading needs, and the u-sweep is the honest test of whether a two-state description is threshold-robust at all. *Adds:* consistency-table content — 246 s against a measured 22–25 s circling period. *Pitfall:* the ⟨Δz²⟩ plateau is set by the altitude band, which is *not* stationary within a flight (the boundary layer deepens through the day), so the approach-to-plateau shape is confounded until the solar-time covariate exists — quote the saturation *time*, not the approach law, until then.

**7.3 v_z spectrum, as a dwell-time statement only.** VALUABLE — f·P(f) with nperseg ≥ 4096 s, pooled by the per-frequency **median**, fitted to Lorentzian vs power law. *Adds:* not a cycle estimator — there is no peak; what it does give is the measured f^−1.2 (32–256 s) steepening to f^−1.6 (128–512 s), flatly inconsistent with a single exponential dwell time and consistent with a broad one, plus a spectral gap near 64 s separating the filter/turbulence band from the cycle band. *Pitfall:* nperseg=512 as originally specified resolves nothing below 1/512 Hz and would report a spurious period; require segments ≥ 3·nperseg (~38,000 para / ~1,150 hang).

**7.4 Joint (v̄_z, |Ω̄|) state density.** VALUABLE — turn rate Ω=(v_E a_N−v_N a_E)/|v|² from stored accelerations, coarse-grained at the same w. *Adds:* the v_z marginal is at best marginally bimodal at every w (separation peaks at 1.90 at w=20 s, 1.60–1.72 over 30–240 s, never reaching 2; Sarle's coefficient peaks at 0.404 against a 0.555 threshold; ΔBIC=2202 is worthless at n≳10⁵) — the second coordinate is free, independent of the vertical noise, and breaks the degeneracy. *Pitfall:* dt ≤ 2 s only; at dt ≥ 5 s the 5-sample SG window spans more than one circle and Ω aliases to zero.

**7.5 Wind as a stratifier.** VALUABLE — Kåsa circle fit on 60 s sliding hodograph windows, with the **yield audited per cadence** (pass rate 34–44 % at dt≤4 s, 24 % at 5 s, <1.5 % at dt≥15 s), used as a window-level covariate and as median-|w| terciles for recomputing α. *Adds:* sizes the confound — median wind 3.1–3.5 m/s against 14–15 m/s ground speed, advecting ~29 km over a median flight against an 87 km median path, so air-mass advection is first-order and reframes drift centring as a physical decomposition; within-flight CV of |w| is 0.41, so a per-flight scalar wind is a poor summary. *Pitfall:* stratify, never integrate — see §Rejected.

**7.6 Altitude-band conditioning and the per-flight vertical budget.** VALUABLE — TAMSD local slope conditioned on normalised height within the flight's own band; and per-flight climb total, climb fraction (~0.455), mean climb rate and sink total regressed against that flight's straightness. *Adds:* the first covariate that varies *within* a flight, and the first *continuous* physical covariate for the heterogeneity test, converting a categorical comparison into a regression. *Pitfall:* the regression needs a per-flight exponent, which is not estimable (see §Rejected) — regress against straightness and the §3.4 exit-time scale instead, or use a hierarchical model.

**7.7 Soft-weighted climb/glide/cross decomposition of the horizontal MSD.** OPTIONAL — s(t)=σ(v̄_z/σ_z) ∈ [0,1], expanding ⟨|Δr|²⟩ into climb, glide and cross terms. *Adds:* the only decomposition of the measured curve itself; the cross term is the direct evidence that glides are directionally persistent *through* thermals. *Pitfall:* heaviest item in the family (~3× the TAMSD cost); σ_z must be per flight, since convective strength varies by an order of magnitude across days.

## §8 — Synthesis

**8.1 Joint generative model with posterior predictive checks.** ESSENTIAL *(examiner's addition)* — a Lévy-walk-with-rests / run-and-tumble with a broad tumble-time distribution, three or four parameters, fitted jointly (simulation-based inference on the §4.1 simulator) to a subset of curves, then **predicting curves held out of the fit** (the TAMSD, the VACF, the turning-angle joint, the excursion distribution, R_p(Δ)). *Adds:* the only procedure that converts "how many regimes" from a curve-reading into a falsifiable statement, and the only one that uses the agreement between families as evidence rather than as decoration. *Pitfall:* the model must be fitted to within-segment observables only; fitting it to the §1 ensemble curve reintroduces the population landmarks as physics.

**8.2 Consistency table, ~8 rows, with pre-declared tolerances.** ESSENTIAL — each row: two independent routes to the same quantity, a **pre-declared tolerance and effect size**, and clustered intervals. Rows: H from filtered variations p=2 vs wavelet variance; H vs 1/z from moving-origin exit times; §3.2 breakpoint vs §6.2 VACF/heading decorrelation time; §3.2 breakpoint vs §7.2 cycle scale; front speed vs measured v_max; R_p flatness vs breakpoint positions; single-particle vs pair-dispersion exponent (§4.2); split-half agreement on K and τ (P1). *Pitfall:* the 15-row design carries no information — with the current ±0.001 bars almost every row reads "inconsistent" for statistical reasons, with honest bars almost every row reads "consistent" for the opposite one. Rows without a pre-declared tolerance are dropped, not softened.

## Explicitly OUT of scope — requires segmentation (Chapter 4)

- Hard climb/glide state labels, and any per-state exponent, per-state MSD or per-state velocity statistic.
- The waiting-time density ψ(τ) *per state*, and the CTRW/Lévy-walk parameter identification that depends on it (§7.2's excursion distribution is the thresholdless surrogate, and it is not the same object).
- Attribution of the horizontal exponent to a flight phase (§7.7's soft decomposition is the honest partial answer available now).
- Transition-rate modelling, HMM/HSMM state dwell distributions, and any state-transition matrix; §6.6 only decides whether that object is well posed.
- Per-thermal statistics (climb rate by thermal, thermal spacing as a measured length rather than as an inferred crossover scale).
- Renewal testing of the *runs* (order-shuffle surrogates on run durations) — the runs do not exist until they are defined by a segmentation.

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| **Multifractal spectra: MFDFA h(q,s), wavelet leaders c₁/c₂, curvature of ζ(q)/qν(q) on the pooled archive** | A mixture of monofractals *is* multifractal, and 40 % (wing class) / 62 % (orographic) departure is already measured; 1.72 dec cannot separate curved from linear ζ(q), and leaders need ≥8 clean octaves. Survives only within a stratum, q ≤ 3, as 5.6 — never as "the main test". |
| **Local Whittle / ELW and GPH** | The periodogram of a drifting 4096 s trajectory is dominated by the drift (a linear trend gives d = ½ exactly); ELW removes one *linear* trend but the real course turns. The advertised 1/(4m) variance assumes independent flights, which day×site clustering and gaggles contradict — the single most misleading precision claim in the inventory. |

DFA-2 inside a window comparable to a task leg removes the glide itself. Filtered variations (2.1) and wavelet variance (2.2) give the same detrending with better range at a fraction of the cost. |
